# v2-genealogy — Round 2 critique (cold seed investor)

Date: 2026-11-03. Variant: `variants/v2-genealogy.md`. Files read, in order: SCORECARD.md, the variant, every file in `research/` and `research/assumptions/`, and `variants/current.md`. No CHANGELOG, no other v2 variant, no other critique.

## Stance

Cold seed investor; default is no. I care about who pays, how much, why now, the wedge, the distribution, what must be true for a $1B outcome, and whether the founder is fooling themselves about platform risk. I am allergic to storage economics (this variant avoids them — credit), to consumer products that need behaviour change (this one mostly doesn't — credit), and to small channels dressed up as markets (this one is a small market dressed up honestly, which is better, but it is still small). Ceiling, Distribution and Platform risk are scored hardest, per the brief.

## Failed claims found before scoring

The variant was told to cite tagged inputs. Five of its numbers are ones the research files themselves have **withdrawn or corrected**, and the variant re-uses them with a "sourced-secondary" tag as if they stood:

1. **"3.6M at the 2020 close [sourced-secondary: Blackstone 2020-12-04 via genealogy-market.md Q1]"** — genealogy-market.md Q1 and its audit log say the 3.6M figure "is therefore **withdrawn**: it rested on a search snippet, and the two primary pages are unreachable." The variant's "flat for five years (3.6M → 3.8M → over three million)" falsification line rests on it. The research's own reading is "no disclosed growth," not "flat."
2. **"NGS 'nearly 10,000' members [sourced-secondary: NGS fact sheet Dec 2018 via genealogy-market.md Q2]"** — genealogy-market.md Q2: "the 'nearly 10,000 (2018)' figure in an earlier draft had no URL and is withdrawn." Used in the variant's Customer and Distribution sections.
3. **"Family Tree Maker ~$79.95 [sourced-secondary]"** — withdrawn in genealogy-market.md Q2 ("had no URL and is withdrawn"). Minor, but it is the pattern.
4. **"Legacy Family Tree Webinars… '220+ live classes' in 2026 [sourced-secondary: genealogybargains.com]"** — genealogy-market.md Q5: "The earlier '220+ live classes, 117 expert presenters' line had no URL and is withdrawn." The primary shows 23 upcoming live webinars.
5. **"RootsMagic grew 'from a simple 10×10′ booth in 2016 to a 30×40′ space' [sourced-secondary: blog.rootsmagic.com ?p=4048] — the channel works for a $39.95 product."** genealogy-market.md audit log: the booth-growth sentence was **misattributed**; it belongs to The Family History Guide Association, a nonprofit, not a vendor. The inference ("the channel works for a $39.95 product") therefore has no support.

One more: Why-now #1 uses Ancestry's "largest acquisition in terms of revenue" quote. digitization-market.md Q3 marks it "**unverified** and not used as evidence" (Fast Company 403 twice).

None of these breaks the thesis on its own; together they mean the variant's Customer section was assembled from the pre-audit draft of genealogy-market.md. I score what the research actually supports.

## Most damaging objection

The entire paying pool (≈3–4.8M) lives inside two companies — Ancestry and MyHeritage — that already ship era dating (Photo Insights 2026-08-25; PhotoDater, free), deceased-face matching (Face Match; Photo Tagger), bulk tagging, and a cross-user "someone else has a photo of your ancestor" loop (Photo Discoveries 2026-07-21); own the tree the product attaches to; closed their APIs (Ancestry 2023-12 and again 2026-05-05); and sell the only genealogy photo subscription that exists ($49.90, with unlimited storage the variant does not match at $59.99). The variant's differentiator is that it will name *living* relatives with consent where the incumbents name none — a policy line Ancestry can cross with a help-center edit the moment it decides per-subject e-consent is worth the exposure. Meanwhile the one standalone photo product in this segment (MyHeritage Photo plan) has never disclosed take-up, MyHeritage retired its last paid AI photo add-on in January 2026, and the closest independent comparable (Related Faces, $99/yr, FamilySearch-listed, TPM sponsor since 2023) publishes zero customers. I am being asked to fund a $60/yr add-on sold one at a time into walled gardens, through a storefront those gardens' free cousin controls, at a ceiling the author concedes is $8–30M. That is a feature with a flat host, not a company.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2).** The honest number ($8–30M) is in the variant's own text; the inputs beneath it (3.6M withdrawn, MyHeritage 1M undated, attach rate [unknown], $70 blended price an assumption) do not survive the research files they cite. Distribution (3) and Platform risk (3) are structurally the same problem: the pool, the API, the storefront and the tree all belong to someone else.

## Three questions for the founder

1. Name thirty Ancestry or MyHeritage subscribers who will pay $60/yr on top of $238–538 for this, and tell me why each of them is not already on MyHeritage's $49.90 Photo plan, which includes unlimited scanning and storage that you do not offer. What did the pre-sale page convert at?
2. If FamilySearch refuses certification, or takes twelve months, what is day-1 value with a tree and no reference faces — and what share of clusters did the week-4 corpora actually resolve? Show me the three genealogists' numbers, not the thresholds.
3. Ancestry now owns the scanning funnel (iMemories), has shipped dating (Photo Insights), and names the dead (Face Match). What is your evidence that "consent-based naming of living relatives" is a thing genealogists will pay for, rather than a legal constraint you have rebranded as a feature — and what happens to this company the week Ancestry's help-center sentence "only designed to help identify deceased people" disappears?
