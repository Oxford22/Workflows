## Stance

I am 47. My mother is 78 and lives 600 miles away. Two years ago I gave her Storyworth; she answered 9 of 52 prompts and no book was printed. There is a Wi-Fi frame in her kitchen. I spend $100–200 on her at Mother's Day and at Christmas. Three shoeboxes of her prints are in my closet, and she is the only person alive who can name the people in them.

This is the first variant in the project aimed at something I would say out loud. That is why I score it hard: I am the bullseye, I have already paid once for this job, and the purchase failed for a reason this variant does not remove.

## How I get to each of the three hard scores

**Pain 5.** The pain is real for me and it has a deadline. But look at what I actually did about it: I paid $99 once, it produced nine answers, and I did nothing for two years. I have never photographed a single print from those boxes, and I have never sat her down with a voice recorder, which costs nothing. The research says I am typical: about 10 stories per Remento family against 52 prompts [estimated: memoir-gift-market.md Q2], "a stalled subscription is the most common bad outcome" [sourced-secondary: memoir-gift-market.md Q1], and when naming is left to the owner, 0.10 tags per scan [estimated: digitization-market.md Q6]. The pain that reliably opens my wallet is "what do I give her that means something", twice a year. That pain is already served at $99 by a company with a million books behind it.

**Wedge 4.** My first ten minutes are not ten minutes. Step 2 offers 20 photos from my phone picker, but the photos I care about are prints in a closet, so I am photographing fronts and backs in a mobile browser. Twenty is an evening; 300 is a weekend I have been not spending for years. Then the product stops and waits for my mother. Because consent has to come from her, she must place the first call, to a number on a card, to a machine. This is the same woman who did 9 of 52, and whom I have personally told never to talk to robot callers. The variant's own kill line is "more than 30% of gifted parents never place the first inbound call within 30 days" and nothing in research/ says which side of it she lands on: the only study of talking to a voice assistant about one's own photos had 13 people with a mean age of 65.8 [sourced: memoir-gift-market.md Q5]. A photo is a better prompt than "tell me about your first job"; I believe that. I do not believe she will ring a robot on her own. In practice the first call happens when I am standing in her kitchen at Christmas, which means the product's activation is me.

**Distribution 3.** I would find this by searching "Storyworth alternative" in late April and landing in a page of vendor-written comparisons, Storyworth's own among them [memoir-gift-market.md Q8]. The two small products closest to this one have not been found by people like me: Memory Murals has 100+ Android downloads, Tell Mel's traction is [unknown] after nearly two years [memoir-gift-market.md Q3–Q4]. The loop the variant counts on is plausible in one respect: if I got a two-minute clip of my mother naming the woman on the left, I would forward it to my brother within the minute. Whether he then buys one for his mother-in-law is [unknown], and the clip only exists if my mother made the call. Storyworth meanwhile buys national television for Mother's Day, Father's Day and the holidays [sourced: memoir-gift-market.md Q1, iSpot and Tatari]; the variant lists only its podcast ads.

## Where the variant and the repaired research disagree (research wins)

1. **Storyworth revenue "~$13.5–45M a year" and "150,000–500,000 plans a year".** Withdrawn after audit; Storyworth revenue and customers are [unknown] (memoir-gift-market.md Q1). Everything the variant builds on it fails as sourced: category of 210,000–560,000 gifts and $20–52M, "0.34–0.91% of people 65+", Remento at "12–39% of Storyworth's volume", the 10,500–84,000 gift range and its $1.6–12.5M, and "$100M = 1.1–3.6 times Storyworth's volume". The direction survives on the research's own line: $100M at about $90 net needs about 1.1M plans a year, roughly the floor of all books Storyworth has printed since 2013.
2. **"~16 stories per Remento order".** Withdrawn; the same-period figure is about 10 per family. Completion is worse than the variant says.
3. **"~40 stories per Storyworth book" as a completion proxy.** The research calls it a ratio of two undated floors, not a completion rate.
4. **"None runs an interviewer over the family's photos and emits a per-photo record."** Narrowed: Memory Murals tags people and dates automatically from voice; Remento's ornament attaches every recording to one photo; a 2024 prototype did voice conversations over a user's photos with a people-places-time graph (memoir-gift-market.md Q4). What is missing is only the combination with a phone call and a book.
5. **Memory Murals at "$69/year founding price".** Research records $99.99 a year or $12.99 a month.
6. **Remento "Legacybox digitisation partnership around May 2025 [memoir-gift-market.md Q2]".** Not in the repaired file. Unsupported.
7. **"43% of 75+ owned a smartphone in 2021".** Withdrawn, no URL; the 75+ split is [unknown] (family-artifact-market.md Q5). This is my mother's age band, so the 40% booklet share is a guess on a guess.
8. **"Famileo: 70% of subscriptions from word of mouth".** The research dates that sentence to before 2022, when Famileo had 700,000 users. It is not current evidence.
9. **Voice cost $7.2–27.9, central $12.5.** The research range with retries and chapter writing is about $9–67, and about $19–30 on mid-priced stacks. On those figures gross margin is about 34–41%, not 46% [estimated: 81.2 − 12.5 + 19.3 or 30.2, over 149], and the variant's falsifier ("voice above $28") sits inside the research's mid-range.
10. **Magic Interviews "on the $199 tier".** The research records a conflict: Storyworth's own blogs put it on the $99 Color plan too. If so, I can get a phone interviewer for $99 from the company I already have an account with.
11. **Institutions "$7.5–28.3M at 8.4% penetration".** The $8–28M range was withdrawn; the repaired span is about $1.7M–85M and the file says arithmetic does not cap it, behaviour does. The variant's $0–2M line is unaffected.
12. **Not in research/ at all** (variant's own fetches): FCC 24-17, Pew on unknown callers, Blurb and USPS prices, state recording-consent counts. I have treated them as the variant's claims, not as research findings.

One sensitivity the variant does not run: if I will only pay the category's $99, revenue per gift is about $113 and first-order contribution about $33 [estimated: 99 + 13.8 − (81.2 − 1.45)], so the podcast break-even doubles to about 0.76 orders per 1,000 downloads [estimated: 25 ÷ 33].

## Most damaging objection

**The product's first step belongs to the one person who has already shown she will not take it, and the only new remedy on offer is one my existing vendor already sells.**

My Storyworth failed at my mother, not at me. v3-gift keeps the same structure (I pay, she performs for a year, the book comes at the end) and adds a harder opening move for her: phone a machine before anything happens. The category's one completion number says about 10 of 52 [memoir-gift-market.md Q2]; mine was 9. No source in research/ shows people over 70 completing scheduled AI phone interviews at product scale. Meanwhile the thing that is supposed to fix completion, a phone interviewer with follow-up questions that works on a landline, shipped at Storyworth in 2026 with photo prompts beside it, and Storyworth keeps my account open after the plan year [memoir-gift-market.md Q1]. So as a burned buyer I have three options: do nothing (what I have done for two years), try Storyworth's phone feature on the account I already own, or pay a company I have never heard of 30–50% more for the same bet, in a category where 8 of the 17 named companies are closed, insolvent, pivoted or of doubtful status [memoir-gift-market.md Q8]. The per-photo record is what I want. It is not enough to make me pay twice for a promise that depends on my mother.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling, 2.** The variant says $2–24M itself, and the upper half of that range stands on a withdrawn estimate. Of the three dimensions I was asked to score hardest, Distribution (3) is the weakest and Wedge (4) is the one that decides whether anything else matters.

## Strongest dimension

**Buildability, 6.** For the first time in this project nothing waits on Apple or Google: no library permission, no migration, no background entitlement. Hosted voice models are priced and on sale [memoir-gift-market.md Q5], and two companies already place AI calls to elders [Q3], so the four-week demo is real. From my side the best thing in the spec is the day-1 clip: one photo, her voice, the names. That is the only moment in the variant I would show another person, and it can be tested with ten mothers before anything else is built.

## What I give up, who I trust, what happens if you die

- I upload family photos and hand over my mother's phone number and her recorded voice. No voiceprint and no face template is a good answer [face-regulation.md Q7]; the variant itself marks a stored face box plus a name as untested.
- I prepay for a book that prints up to 12 months later. "Cash arrives up to 12 months before the book is printed" is my money at risk, not only a seasonality note.
- "Free to read forever" from a company with a one-off sale and a 34–46% margin is not a promise I can price. HereAfter's site returns 404; Artifact hosted "through 2024" and stopped [memoir-gift-market.md Q3].
- The export (photos with embedded names, audio files, text) is the right answer, and it is the reason the asset score is not higher: once I have it, I am done.
- Versus today: $129–149 against the $99 I already know, plus my hours with a shoebox. Inside my gift budget; not inside my patience.
- Would I recommend it? Only after a book with correct names arrived. Before that I would say what I say about Storyworth: nice idea, she didn't do it.
