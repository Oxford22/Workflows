# v2-grandparent — Multigenerational family feed + archive (grandparent loop)

Written 2026-11-03 for round 2. Relates to `variants/current.md` (v1-organizer + index-only engine) and to the sibling round-2 variant `variants/v2-prints.md` (the grandparents' box of prints is this variant's second payer). Method note: the shared WebSearch budget for this turn was exhausted after the research files were read; every new web fact below comes from a direct WebFetch of a known URL (cdc.gov, family-album.com, mixi.co.jp, tinybeans.com, asx.com.au, bellababyphotography.com; mom365.com and babylist.com/about returned 503 / no figures), and the gaps that needed a search are marked [unknown] with what was tried.

## One line

A new-baby app whose first act is to find today's photos of the baby in the parents' existing iCloud/Google camera rolls (indexed in place, nothing re-uploaded, nothing stored by us but previews) and send them to the grandparents on both sides, who view in a browser with no install; the same engine then names the grandparents' own old prints, indexes every relative's library where it already lives, and becomes the one cross-platform, three-generation family search with consent-aware faces and a BYO-AI endpoint — the archive that outlives the feed FamilyAlbum and Tinybeans cannot keep past the child's fifth birthday.

## Wedge (first use case adopted without changing habits; the first 10 minutes; day-1 value for ONE user; day-7 value)

**No habit change.** The parent already photographs the baby into the iPhone/Android camera roll (and already pays iCloud/Google for it: ~60% of households [estimated: sizing.md row 17]) and already wants the grandparents to see today's pictures; 65% of Japanese parents install an app for exactly this at the birth [sourced: MIXI 2026-05-07]. The only habit we remove is the second upload. No library migration (SCORECARD rule), no Takeout, no Mac: PhotoKit `.authorized` gives full-library read on iPhone [sourced: research/platform-access.md Q1]; Android `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES` with a core-functionality declaration (a baby-photo app is the textbook case) [sourced: research/memory-product-sdk.md Q6; platform-access.md Q8].

**The first 10 minutes (one parent, one phone).**
1. Create the family; name the baby; pick 5 photos of her. On-device face embedding (AdaFace, MIT [sourced: research/ai-why-now.md Q3]) plus capture time/location seeds a "baby" cluster. Templates never leave the phone (research/face-regulation.md Q7 rule 1); the parent/guardian consents for the minor (rule 2(b)).
2. The app scans only the *recent* roll (last 90 days, a few hundred items at ~200 photos/month [estimated: unit-economics.md Q2]) — seconds, not the 38–57-minute full-library session the round-1 builders measured for 30k photos [sourced: research/ios-feasibility.md Q3]. Backfill of the whole library is an opt-in later step run on charger.
3. "14 photos of Mia today — send to Grandma Ruth and Grandpa Joe?" Parent taps once. Relatives get a text/email link; they open a browser page (no account, no install, the FamilyAlbum pattern) and see the photos as display-size derivatives (not originals) served from our previews bucket. They can tap "that's me" on their own face later (consent) and reply with a voice note.
4. A SigLIP 2 (Apache-2.0 [sourced: research/unknowns-r1.md Q5]) embedding and a face cluster are kept on-device per parent; the shared derivative plus caption go to the family index; originals stay in iCloud/Google.

**Day-1 value for ONE user (the parent).** The daily job — pick, upload, send — becomes a one-tap review of what the phone already found; the grandparents see the baby in minutes; nothing was re-uploaded, re-encoded or capped at 2 minutes. Honest: this is a convenience gain over FamilyAlbum's manual upload, not a new job. Whether parents value "auto-found" over "I chose" is [unknown] until the pilot (assumption 2). Single-parent day-1 value without relatives is modest: Apple/Google already show "Mia" as a People album on the parent's own phone.

**Day-7 value.** Both grandparent households are in the feed (the loop); the parent's partner (often on the other platform) has joined and her phone is indexed in place too — the first cross-platform family index; Grandma has scanned the first album page of the parent's own baby photos ("who is this?" → named by Grandma, consent screen for the living, deceased flag for the rest — the v2-prints flow); the family can ask "show me Mia with Grandpa Joe" and "show me her mother at the same age", which no feed app answers [sourced: FamilyAlbum search is comment-based, above; Tinybeans "AI-driven photo organisation" is in development, no launch date, Q3 FY26 via customers.md Q1(h)]. The Claude install link exposes the family index as a custom connector (Free tier, one connector, mobile use) [sourced: research/assumptions/A6.md For #1]; a checkbox, not the reason to buy [sourced: research/byo-ai.md §3 verdict].

The Q1 FY27 4C (2026-10-28) exists but is gated [unknown: ASX terms page].
2. **2026-05-07 / 2026-05-15: the largest operator turned the dial from growth to margin.** FamilyAlbum crossed 30M; FY2027 guidance for the Lifestyle segment is to "maintain" the paying rate; the Integrated Report says that from FY2027 emphasis shifts "from user acquisition to investment efficiency", and ads were introduced after ~10 years because "a stronger revenue base was essential" to keep serving 30M users free [sourced: MIXI 2026-05-07; 決算短信 2026-05-15; Integrated Report 2026 via grandparent-loop.md Q1]. Segment margin 5.1% [estimated: ¥876M ÷ ¥17,159M]. The free-unlimited-storage incumbent is monetising harder, not expanding the product.
3. **2026-06-08: Apple opened iCloud Shared Albums to Android/Windows with full-resolution contributions** [sourced: macrumors 2026-06-08 via customers.md Q1(c)] — the platforms are moving toward each other at the album level, which both validates the cross-platform family job and narrows it (counter-signal, held).
4. **2026-08-07: G.T. v. Samsung (7th Cir.)** — face templates computed and kept on the user's device, inaccessible to the company, are outside BIPA "possess/collect" [sourced: Mayer Brown 2026-08-20 via research/face-regulation.md Q1]; 2026-09-30 Doe v. Apple: the iCloud Faceprint subclass survived on a theory that reaches E2E-encrypted sync, and "BIPA has no encryption exception" [sourced-secondary: ppc.land 2026-10-10; Ashurst 2026-10-23 via unknowns-r1.md Q8]. On-device-only templates are now the litigated safe design; synced templates are now the litigated unsafe one.
5. **2026-09-14: iOS 27 Foundation Models with Claude/Gemini as providers and tool calling over the user's existing library; Siri AI voice search (English-first)** [sourced: research/assumptions/A4.md Against #5; CHANGELOG round 0 item 4]. Any app can now run a third-party model over PhotoKit — a gift to every developer, which is why the moat here is the family graph, not the AI.
6. **2025-02-24: Apple relicensed MobileCLIP/MobileCLIP2 weights research-only; google/siglip2 is Apache-2.0** [sourced: huggingface.co commit 993166d; siglip2 model card via unknowns-r1.md Q5]. The on-device embedding stack for a commercial app is settled.
7. **2026-06-16 (AARP) and Jan–Jun 2026 (Pew): grandparents are reachable.** 76% use electronic technology as a primary connection; 79% of 65+ own a smartphone (up from 55% of 50+ in 2016 to 90% in 2025 per AARP Tech Trends) [sourced: grandparent-loop.md Q4].
8. **Caption cost collapse**: Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite $0.0000628/image, Claude Haiku 5.5 $0.000123 at 768 px [sourced: research/unit-economics.md Q3, price pages 2026-11-03]; irrelevant to margin at feed volumes, relevant to "ask the archive".
9. **Counter-signals (held):** Google Photos partner sharing is still one partner and face labels are "private to each account" with no change in the Oct 2025–Oct 2026 blog feed [sourced: support.google.com/photos/answer/6128838 and /7378858; research/assumptions/A3.md For #1]; Apple does not share People names across Shared Library participants [sourced: unknowns-r1.md Q1]; Immich shipped cross-user people free on 2026-10-07 [sourced: A3.md Against #3]. No platform has moved on the family-faces job — which cuts both ways: the gap is open, and nobody with distribution has judged it worth a slide. A single Oct-2026 Google Play review complaining FamilyAlbum "added AI photo search" is unverified [sourced-secondary: grandparent-loop.md Q1]; the Premium page shows no AI search on 2026-11-03.

## Product (core loop; explicitly NOT in v1)

**Core loop.**
1. **Find** (parent's phone, on-device): new photos → face cluster + time/location → "today's photos of Mia" card. Backfill of the full library opt-in, on charger, foreground session (Camera use kills the task; expiry is final) [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q2].
2. **Send** (one tap): display derivatives (~300 KB) + caption + confirmed names to the family index; originals stay in iCloud/Google. Grandparents view in a browser via magic link; app optional.
3. **React**: comments, reactions, voice notes — MIXI's own gating KPI is "reactions on photos" [sourced: Integrated Report via grandparent-loop.md Q1].
4. **Join** (relative's phone, optional): index in place; cross-member face suggestions (adult↔adult is solved: AdaFace AgeDB-30 98% [sourced: ai-why-now.md Q3]); confirm/reject only; consent screen per living named person (BIPA 15(b) e-signature; Everalbum "separate and apart" disclosure) [sourced: face-regulation.md Q1, Q5].
5. **Scan** (grandparent's prints): the v2-prints phone-scan flow — fronts, backs, "who is this?" cards, decade estimate — writes named JPEG+XMP into the grandparent's own library and into the family index [sourced: variants/v2-prints.md Wedge; ios-feasibility.md Q9].
6. **Ask**: natural-language search across three generations and two platforms; MCP endpoint for the family's assistant (commodity: 54,763 active registry servers [sourced: byo-ai.md Q5]).
7. **Leave**: XMP `PersonInImage` + MWG regions, Apple keywords, Google `appendonly` upload — "portable names, not portable People albums" [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q9; platform-access.md Q4].

**Explicitly NOT in v1.** Storing originals (ever by default; optional cold-tier vault later); free unlimited uploads; prints/photobooks fulfilment (partner links only — see Business model); automatic cross-generation face matching (child→adult across decades is open research: 71% TAR at an 8-year gap [sourced: ai-why-now.md Q3]); server-side face matching anywhere; naming living people without subject consent; Android *capture* app at launch (Android relatives use the browser; the Android indexer is v1.1 — the round-1 federation critics' Android objection is answered by the web feed, not by parity); GPS trackers, stickers, ads (MIXI's "economic sphere" lines).

## Economics (per-customer COGS, GM, payback; cite research/unit-economics.md rows or derive with tags)

Reference family: 2 parent phones + 1 relative phone indexed in place; ~1,000 shared photos/yr at ~300 KB display derivatives; 6 relatives in the browser feed.

- **Indexing**: on-device embeddings and faces, $0 [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q3 throughput; unit-economics.md Q3(d) server alternative $0.03–0.07 per 22k images not incurred]. Vector hosting $0.05/user/mo (disk-resident pgvector) × 3 = $0.15/mo [sourced: unit-economics.md Q4]; incremental indexing $0.007 × 3 = $0.02/mo [sourced: unit-economics.md Q4].
- **Feed derivatives**: 1,000 × 300 KB = 0.3 GB/yr on R2 at $0.015/GB-mo, egress free ≈ $0.005/mo [estimated: unit-economics.md Q1 rate]; captions for shared photos only, 1,000 × $0.0000628 = $0.06/yr [estimated: unit-economics.md Q3 Flash-Lite row]. Notifications (SMS/email) ~$0.10/mo [estimated: assumption].
- **Total variable COGS ≈ $0.33/mo ≈ $4/yr**; consistent with the A-index-only row ($0.16/mo per single user, 86% GM) [sourced: unit-economics.md Q7].
- **Payment**: Stripe 2.9% + $0.30 on $59 = $2.01; App Store SBP 15% = $8.85 [sourced: unit-economics.md Q7].
- **Support**: $4/family/yr [estimated: assumption — consumer, non-technical grandparents on the web side; FamilyAlbum's grandparent-cancellation note and Legacybox's billing-complaint pattern say this is the real COGS and it is a guess].
- **Contribution on $59**: web ≈ $59 − 4 − 2.01 − 4 = **$49 (83%)**; in-app ≈ **$42 (71%)** [estimated: sums above]. Archive tier at $99 with print scanning (cloud OCR/dating pass $0.12 per 1,000 scans [estimated: v2-prints.md Economics]) ≈ $88 (89%) web.
- **Payback**: CAC $35 → 8.6 months; $50 → 12 months; $90 → 22 months [estimated: CAC ÷ ($49/12)]. Max tolerable CAC: 15.6-month life (RevenueCat P&V) ≈ $53 [sourced: unit-economics.md Q7 A-index-only column]; Qeepsake-shaped 4.8-year life ≈ $235 at LTV/CAC 1; Backblaze-like 133-month life $456 [sourced: same table].
- **Versus FamilyAlbum's cost structure**: MIXI stores originals (S3 Standard-IA → Glacier IR 2022) plus smartphone-optimised and S/M/L thumbnails, generates HLS video on demand, and still needed ads and a games-business subsidy to reach a 5.1% segment margin [sourced: MIXI SRE talk 2023-06-07; Integrated Report via grandparent-loop.md Q6]. Index-only cannot be the system of record, and that is the trade: we never carry the storage bill, and we never get to promise "we keep everything forever" (assumption 1).
- **What breaks at scale**: nothing on compute; support for two grandparent households per family; consent-flow tickets ("Grandpa never tapped 'that's me'"); BIPA/GDPR exposure if any template ever reaches a server (face-regulation.md risk table). Face vectors are the one thing the family index must not hold.

## Platform risk (the obvious feature Apple/Google/incumbent could ship; why this survives or doesn't)

- **FamilyAlbum (the real incumbent).** Could ship camera-roll auto-pick (it already runs face recognition for per-child pages and "Pages by child") and AI search in two releases; 30M users, 380K iOS ratings, a profitable segment, ¥40B/>20%-EBITDA target [sourced: grandparent-loop.md Q1, Q3]. Survival argument: MIXI's economics are built on free unlimited storage of originals (Glacier IR, HLS, ads) and its growth lines are GPS trackers, prints and stickers — "life-stage adjacency, not archive" — and its FY2027 posture is "maintain paying rate" and investment efficiency [sourced: Integrated Report; 決算短信]. Indexing the parents' *existing* libraries and the grandparents' prints is a different product with a different cost base, not a feature on a feed. Honest: that is a business-model argument; technically MIXI could do it, and the US is only ~20% of its users.
- **Tinybeans** is already pivoting to our positioning with ~95k paid, "organic growth has stalled", a −97% share price since 2021, A$1.64M cash and a new Head of Product [sourced: grandparent-loop.md Q2; ASX listing 2026-10-12]. It is as much a warning as a competitor: the "multigenerational archive" message did not move its paid base (49,796 → 49,793 Q2→Q3 FY26 [sourced-secondary: deck snippet via grandparent-loop.md Q2]). What it has not done is index in place; what it has done is price the post-feed years at a 47% discount (Legacy $39.99) [estimated: grandparent-loop.md Q2].
- **Apple**: iCloud Shared Photo Library (up to 6, one library at a time, creator pays storage, Apple-only, People names not shared across participants on the evidence found) [sourced: customers.md Q1(c); unknowns-r1.md Q1]; Shared Albums now reachable from Android (2026-06-08). ## Compounding asset

- **The three-generation identity graph**: who is who across the parents' libraries, both grandparent households and the scanned prints — confirmed by humans, consented by subjects, re-derivable by nobody from a single export. Grows with every birth, every relative who joins, every print named. Deliberately exported (XMP), so the lock-in is the *graph across households*, not the files.
- **Consent ledger** for living named people — an asset no incumbent holds (face-regulation.md Q6: nobody obtains consent from the people in the photos).
- **The feed history as provenance**: reactions, voice notes, "Grandma says this was the lake house, 1983" — the MIXI KPI (reactions) turned into archive metadata.
- **Family-level network**: each new birth inside an enrolled family lands in an existing archive (the backward loop); the grandparents are the persistent node across their children's families.
- Honest: embeddings and the index are rebuildable for cents [sourced: unit-economics.md Q3]; MCP is a commodity [sourced: byo-ai.md Q5]; the feed itself has zero switching cost until the archive beneath it is large. The asset compounds at the rate of consent, which is the rate the un-named share (19–46% in the worked scenario [estimated: face-regulation.md Q7]) comes down.

## Buildability (4-week demo spec for 2 engineers; ugliest technical risk)

- **Week 1 — Find (iOS)**: PhotoKit change observation over the recent roll; AdaFace via Core ML (latency on infant faces [unknown]; measure) + time/location clustering; "today's photos of Mia" card with one-tap approve; derivative generation (300 KB) and upload; SigLIP 2 Base on-device embeddings (iPhone latency [unknown]; M5 Pro 5.2 ms/image is the only published figure [sourced-secondary: unknowns-r1.md Q5]).
- **Week 2 — Send and React (web)**: magic-link family feed in the browser (no account), reactions, comments, voice notes; SMS/email notifications; "that's me" consent screen with BIPA 15(b) e-signature text and Everalbum-style separate disclosure; deceased flag.
- **Week 3 — Join and Scan**: second phone (partner, iOS) indexed in place; cross-member face suggestions with confirm/reject; the v2-prints scan flow (front/back capture, Vision OCR, cloud VLM fallback, "who is this?" cards) reused for the grandparent; names written as XMP/keywords to the scanner's own library.
- **Week 4 — Ask and Measure**: natural-language search over the family index (SigLIP text tower); Claude custom-connector install link; run ten real families recruited through one birth cohort (a prenatal class or registry list): measure invite→view rate for grandparents within 48 h, grandparent weekly return rate over 4 weeks, parent approvals per day vs photos found, "that's me" consent completion, and infant-cluster precision/recall against parent labels.
- Acceptance [estimated: thresholds chosen against the incumbents' only disclosed numbers]: ≥70% of invited grandparents view within 48 h; ≥50% return weekly in week 4; infant cluster precision ≥90% on day-7 photos; ≥60% of living adults complete consent in 14 days; zero face templates off-device.

**Ugliest technical risk: the baby's face.** Every benchmark in research/ is adults or children ≥2 years (AgeDB-30 98%; YFA 98.5% at a 2-year gap → 71.3% at 8 years [sourced: ai-why-now.md Q3]); infant faces in months 0–12 change fastest and have no published open-model benchmark [unknown]. If the cluster drifts, the "find" step degrades to time-and-location plus a daily parent confirm — still a one-tap review, but the headline evaporates. Second: iOS background. New photos are found only when the parent opens the app (no unattended indexing: BGContinuedProcessingTask is user-initiated, Camera kills it, the background-ANE entitlement has no documented grant path [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q2; unknowns-r1.md Q4]); FamilyAlbum has the same constraint (parents upload manually), so parity, not disadvantage. Third: Android capture is v1.1 (ML Kit GenAI embeddings are foreground-only on flagships; LiteRT SigLIP works on any mid-range SoC) [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q7]. Kill criterion: if week-4 families show grandparent weekly return <30% or parent approvals <1/day, the loop does not spin and the archive never fills.

## Ceiling (path to $100M ARR with tagged inputs and arithmetic; or the honest number it tops out at)

**Pool.** US births 3.63M/yr [sourced: CDC FastStats, 2024 final]; US grandparents 65M, 41M aged 65+ [sourced/estimated: AARP 2026-06-16; sizing.md row 24]; the proven subscription pool: MIXI Lifestyle ≈ $114M/yr [estimated: ¥17,159M ÷ 150] plus Tinybeans $6.5M [sourced-secondary: Medianet 2026-08-27].

**Price.** $59/yr Family [sourced comparable: FamilyAlbum Premium Family $59/yr]; use $60. Blended ARPU per paying family with follower billing and the Archive tier: $60 + (3 relatives × 4.3% × $60 = $7.7) + (30% of paying families with one grandparent household on $99 = $29.7) ≈ **$97** [estimated: k=3 relatives and 30% Archive attach are assumptions; the 4.3% is MIXI's one disclosed loop number].

**Required base.** $100M ÷ $60 = **1.67M concurrently paying families**; ÷ $97 = **1.03M** [estimated].

**Life per paying family, three regimes** (geometric; the plan term and the age-out both matter) [estimated: unit-economics.md Q6 method]:
- (a) RevenueCat Photo & Video annual: 23% first renewal → 1.3 years.
- (b) Qeepsake-shaped: 79% retention → 1/0.21 = 4.8 years.
- (c) Tinybeans+-claimed 96% through the feed years (0–4) then 60% once the child is 5+ (the Legacy-plan age-out, retention assumed): Σ survival = 1 + .96 + .92 + .885 + .85 = 4.6 plus 0.815 ÷ 0.40 = 2.0 → 6.6 years [estimated: assumptions stated; the 96% is an unreconciled vendor KPI].

**Required new paying families per year G = base ÷ life.** At $60: (a) 1.28M/yr = 35% of all US births; (b) 348k/yr = 9.6%; (c) 253k/yr = 7.0%. At $97 blended: (b) 215k = 5.9%; (c) 156k = 4.3% [estimated]. At a Tinybeans-shaped paid conversion of ~10% of active families [estimated: 95k ÷ 1M, vendor inputs; FamilyAlbum's rate [unknown]], the *enrolled* share of births needed is (b) 59–96% and (c) 43–70% — FamilyAlbum-in-Japan penetration (65%), which took 11 years, free unlimited storage, a games subsidy and ads, and which FamilyAlbum has not reached in the US (NA ≈ 6M registered individuals ÷ an assumed 4 per family ÷ ~8 years ≈ 190k families/yr ≈ 5% of births [estimated: 20% × 30M; per-family and tenure divisors are assumptions]). At 25% paid conversion (no operator has disclosed this), enrolment needed is (c) 17–28% of births — Babylist-registrant scale (650k = 18% of births [estimated: 650k ÷ 3.63M]).

**Evidenced corner.** Enrolment 10–20% of births (363–726k families/yr; Tinybeans' cumulative "4M families" over its lifetime implies ~8% of births per year at a 14-year tenure [estimated: 4M ÷ 14; founding year not sourced]) × 10–15% paid → G = 36k–109k/yr × life 4.8–6.6 yr → **175k–720k paying families** × $60–97 → **$10M–$70M**; central (15% enrolled, 12% paid, 5.5-yr life, $80) ≈ **$29M US** [estimated].

**Second payer independent of births.** The Archive tier sold to grandparent households through the print path: 41M grandparents 65+ [estimated] × 1–3% × $99 = **$41M–$122M** [estimated] — but every input is the v2-prints ceiling problem (Photomyne ~402k payers flat; Legacybox ~200k families/yr) and attach rates are [unknown]; v2-prints' own honest number for that pool is $25–45M US [sourced: variants/v2-prints.md Ceiling].

**International.** FamilyAlbum is >40% overseas; Tinybeans is AU/US; Photomyne 28–30% ex-NA [sourced: grandparent-loop.md Q1; digitization-market.md Q1] → +40–70% on the US figure [estimated]; EU requires per-subject explicit consent and on-device-only faces [sourced: face-regulation.md Q4].

**Sensitivity line.** ARR ≈ births 3.63M × enrolled (10–40%) × paid (10–25%) × life (4.8–6.6 yr) × ARPU ($60–97) ≈ **$10M–$234M**; the evidenced corner is $10–70M (central ~$29M) US; the $100M corner needs any two of: enrolment ≥35% of US births (FamilyAlbum-Japan grade, unreached by anyone in the US), paid conversion ≥25% (undisclosed by anyone), or the grandparent Archive tier attaching at ≥2% of all US grandparents 65+ (a print-market outcome above Photomyne's entire base).

**Honest number.** On evidence this is a **$25–45M US ARR** business (birth loop ~$20–35M plus a grandparent Archive line of $5–15M), **$35–75M with international**; the same shape as Tinybeans at 10–20× its scale, and well short of MIXI's ¥17.1B. $100M pencils only as FamilyAlbum's own path — nationwide birth-cohort penetration plus multi-country — run without the free-storage subsidy that produced it. The hinge is the one number nobody discloses: what share of enrolled families pay, and whether the archive holds them past the child's fifth birthday (Qeepsake: churn "as you complete your baby book"; Tinybeans: Legacy at −47%). If the archive turns a feed-app life of ~1.3 years (RevenueCat) into a Backblaze-like system-of-record life (91%/yr), the required G falls to ~150k/yr (4% of births) at $60 and the $100M corner becomes reachable from the evidenced enrolment band — that is the one claim the pilot can test and the one no incumbent has demonstrated.

## Load-bearing assumptions (numbered; each with the observable fact that would falsify it)

1. **Parents will choose index-in-place plus a browser feed over FamilyAlbum's free unlimited re-upload.** Falsified if: in a 200-family prenatal cohort offered both, <20% choose or switch to the entrant within 60 days, or exit interviews cite "it doesn't keep my originals" as the top reason.
2. **"Auto-found today's photos" beats manual selection for parents.** Falsified if: week-4 families approve <1 card/day or disable auto-find within 14 days in >30% of families.
3. **Grandparents view in a browser without installing, and return.** Falsified if: <50% of invited grandparents view within 48 h or <30% return weekly by week 4 (no operator discloses this; the pilot is the only source).
4. **Infant-face clustering holds for months 0–12 on-device.** Falsified if: precision <85% on day-7/day-30/day-90 photos; fallback is time/location + daily confirm.
5. **Paid conversion of enrolled families ≥10%** (Tinybeans-shaped). Falsified if: cohort-1 conversion <6% at 90 days.
6. **The archive retains past the feed years** (life ≥4.8 years). Falsified if: cohort-1 first annual renewal <60%, or year-5 families churn at >50% (Legacy-plan pattern).
7. **The grandparent household converts to the Archive tier at ≥20% of paying families.** Falsified if: <10% attach in the first 1,000 paying families, or <30% of grandparents scan a single album page.
8. **Relatives per family k ≥3 and follower-pay ≈4% (MIXI's one number) hold outside Japan.** Falsified if: k <2 or follower-pay <2% in cohort 1.
9. **Blended CAC ≤$50 via loop + registry.** Falsified if: first 1,000 paying families cost >$70 each, or the registry channel's rev-share exceeds $50/payer.
10. **FamilyAlbum does not ship camera-roll auto-pick + AI search + grandparent print scanning within 12 months.** Falsified by: a FamilyAlbum release note or Premium-page feature list naming any two of these.
11. **Apple/Google do not ship cross-account family People within 12 months.** Falsified by: a WWDC/Google I/O announcement or support-page change (A4 flip condition).
12. **On-device templates, subject consent and a names-and-rectangles index keep the company outside BIPA/CUBI/GDPR Art. 9 possession.** Falsified by: an Illinois state court rejecting G.T. v. Samsung; the Doe v. Apple Faceprint subclass prevailing at trial; an EU DPA decision against consumer face clustering [face-regulation.md Q1, Q4].
13. **The honest ceiling ($25–45M US) is accepted unless assumption 6 proves Backblaze-grade.** Falsified (upward) only by: cohort-1 gross retention ≥85% at 24 months with the archive as the family's system of record.
