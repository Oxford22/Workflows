## 1. Questions asked

1. Paid cloud-photo population: Google One, iCloud+, Amazon Prime (Amazon Photos), Dropbox → US and global households paying for photo-bearing cloud storage.
2. Household structure: mixed iPhone/Android households, multigenerational households, adults with living parents 65+, grandparents → households whose libraries span 2+ accounts or 2 platforms.
3. Library size: photos per user, library GB, video share of bytes, plan fill rate.
4. CAC benchmarks: CPI/CAC for Photo & Video and Lifestyle apps, iOS vs Android; disclosed CAC at Tinybeans/Qeepsake, FamilyAlbum, Ancestry, Backblaze.
5. Search demand for nine target queries, every route tried.
6. Amazon Photos Family Vault: members, shared faces, pooling, 2025–26 changes, AI search.
7. Cloud-to-cloud transfer services as evidence of paying for "move/merge".
8. iCloud Shared Photo Library / Google partner sharing adoption numbers.

Falsification stance: for each population we first looked for evidence the pool is *smaller* than the seed assumed (e.g. that iCloud+ paying share is low, that mixed-OS households are rare, that nobody pays for transfers). Where we found it, it is in the text.

- **Mixed iPhone/Android households: [unknown]** — no survey found. - **Chain B — households whose libraries span 2+ accounts or 2 platforms [estimated]:** any multi-person household with ≥2 smartphone users has ≥2 photo accounts: 94M × (0.91)² ≈ **~78M** (independence assumption; band 76–80M from the household band). ### Q3. Library size

- Phone camera rolls: average "3,139 pictures and videos" (Mixbook/OnePoll, n=1,967, May 2023) [C Q3]; "48% have more than 1,000 photos saved on their phones" and "one in five feel overwhelmed" (Mixbook, n>2,400) [sourced: CNBC 2026-02-28 above]; "the typical person has 1,598 photos on their phone's camera roll", Millennials "just over 2,500" [sourced-secondary: https://kdwb.iheart.com/featured/the-dave-ryan-show/content/2025-02-18-the-average-person-has-1598-photos-n-their-phone-gallery/, 2025-02-18, relaying a NY Post item; sponsor and sample [unknown]; nypost.com not fetchable]. Per-capita capture ~300 photos/yr globally (Rise Above, 2026) [UE Q2]. Falsification: the 1,598 figure is half of Mixbook's 3,139; both are phone rolls, not cloud libraries, and neither reports GB.
- Platform scale: "more than 4 trillion photos are stored in Google Photos" and "every week 28 billion new photos and videos are uploaded" [sourced: https://blog.google/products/photos/storage-changes/, 2020-11-11]. No per-user GB disclosure by Google or Apple: **[unknown]**.
- Self-hosting power users (Immich, Reddit anecdotes via search, 2024–2025; not representative): "48,000 photo / 2750 video / 393 GB"; "Library: 9TiB"; a planning rule "~50–100 GB for 10,000 typical smartphone photos" [sourced-secondary: search summary of r/immich and a hosting guide; Reddit is not fetchable from this session]. - Video share of bytes: **[unknown]** at any rigorous level; UE's secondary bracket is 48–80% of photo+video bytes. Searched again in the first pass (Google/iCloud "percent of storage" video): nothing quantitative.
- Plan fill rate (how full 200 GB plans are): **[unknown]**. Only a roundup-cited "15% of iPhone users have at some point hit the 'iCloud Storage Full' notification" attributed to SellCell [sourced-secondary: https://dblabsapps.com/statistics/iphone-storage-photos-statistics/, 2026; attribution unclear] and a 2025 Cunard-sponsored claim that "over half of Americans have had to delete images due to storage limitations" [sourced-secondary: TechRadar via search; article body did not render]. Neither is usable as a fill rate.
- Derived library bytes [estimated]: 3,139 items × 2.5 MB/photo with 50% of bytes video (UE assumptions) ≈ **~16 GB** for an average phone roll; 1,598 items ≈ 8 GB. A 100 GB "reference user" (UE) is therefore a heavy user or a multi-device/multi-year consolidator, not the median phone owner — this matters for storage COGS and for which plan tier a merged family archive lands on.

### Worked chains

**(a) Multi-account / multi-platform households (US).** ~133M households [est.] − 38.5M one-person [sourced] ≈ 94M multi-person; × 0.91² smartphone [sourced] ≈ 78M with ≥2 photo accounts; × 30–50% mixed-OS [estimated: assumption] ≈ **28–47M cross-platform households**. Paying for cloud storage already (row 17 share 37–82%, central ~60%): **central ~21M cross-platform households that already pay for photo cloud (35M × 0.60); band 10–39M (28M × 0.37 to 47M × 0.82)** [estimated]. $100M ARR at $60/yr = 1.67M households = **~8% of cross-platform payers at the central value (4–16% across the band); ~2.1% of all households with ≥2 accounts**; at $120/yr, half that. For comparison: Backblaze reached 402k consumer customers in "18 plus years of operations" [sourced: 10-K FY2025]; Tinybeans ~95k paid; Ente ~22k paid [UE/C].

**(b) Annual deaths with smartphone libraries (US).** 3,094,593 deaths [sourced: CDC 2026-07-02] × 74.8% aged 65+ [estimated: NCHS db521] = 2.31M; × 79% smartphone (Pew 65+) = 1.83M; plus 0.78M under-65 × 91% = 0.71M → **~2.5M** [estimated]. Haircut: 885k of deaths are 85+, where ownership is below the 65+ average (Pew does not split; AARP gives only 91% for all 50+) — at 60% for the 65+ decedent pool [estimated: assumption — a judgment that 85+ ownership is well below the 79% 65+ average; no published 85+ figure] the total is **~2.1M**. With row 17's paid-cloud share applied to individuals (37–82%, central ~60%) → **~0.8–2.1M, central ~1.3–1.5M paid-cloud libraries orphaned per year** [estimated]. A bereavement-triggered one-time product at $99 would need ~1M purchases/yr (40–50% of the central flow) to reach $100M — not a path to $100M on its own; it is a trigger, not a market.

Estimate: if 60–75% of households hold undigitized prints [estimated: assumption — roughly the share of households whose head was an adult before ~2005, when phone cameras displaced film; no survey] → **80–100M households** [estimated: 133M × 0.60–0.75]; the spend evidence (C Q1(d): $300–1,250 per family, one-time) is for digitization, and the post-scan "merge into the family archive" step is unpriced. **Ceiling arithmetic the variants must confront.** $100M ARR requires 1.67M households at $60/yr or 0.83M at $120/yr [estimated: $100M ÷ price; price band is the assumption stated in Q4]. Pools: 28–47M cross-platform households; ~19–25M multigenerational (Pew definition, bracket 6–37M); ~2.1–2.5M deaths/yr with smartphones; ~80M (50–110M) households already paying for photo cloud.
