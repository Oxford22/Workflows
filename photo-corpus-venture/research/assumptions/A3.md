# A3 — A customer segment exists for whom "merged multi-relative archive, dedupe, faces across generations, ownership that outlives any account" is an acute pain they already pay money or many hours for

Skeptic pass, 2026-11-03. Brief: refute first. Tags per README: `[sourced: url, date]`, `[estimated: method]`, `[unknown]`.

Method note: the shared per-turn WebSearch budget was exhausted before this pass began; all 7 planned refutation searches were refused by the tool. Every new item below therefore comes from direct WebFetch of a primary page (vendor pricing pages, GitHub discussions/releases, Apple Community threads, the HN Algolia site API, The Photo Managers' own blog) plus the existing `research/customers.md` and `research/competitive.md`. Pages that 404/403/503'd are listed at the end so their absence is not mistaken for evidence. Reddit remains unreachable.

## The assumption, as stated in v0

A3: "There exists a customer segment for whom 'merged multi-relative archive' is an acute, paid-for pain." Expanded in this brief to: merged multi-relative archive **+ dedupe + faces across generations + ownership that outlives any account**, already paid for in **money or many hours**.

## Verdict

**partial** — confidence **0.60** that "partial" is the right call (i.e. ~0.25 it is actually broken, ~0.15 it would hold on evidence not yet gathered).

Decomposed:

| Sub-claim | Status | One-line reason |
|---|---|---|
| (i) A segment with this pain exists | holds | Adult children consolidating a parent's/spouse's library, multi-household and cross-platform families, estate/divorce cases, and pro organizers' clients all appear repeatedly in primary forum and vendor sources 2019–2026. |
| (ii) The pain is acute | partial | Acute only at life events (death, divorce, downsizing, a phone/account migration). Between events it is latent: Mixbook's survey says half of smartphone owners "do nothing" with their photos. |
| (iii) They already pay **money** for it | **broken as stated** | Money flows to adjacent jobs (scanning, organizer labour, forward-capture baby apps, genealogy subs). The only merge-specific paid products found are PowerPhotos ($39.95 one-time), Mylio Family ($480–$732/yr) and KinPhotos ($49/yr); none publishes a customer count. No one was found paying for cross-*account* merge with cross-user faces. |
| (iv) They already pay **hours** for it | holds | Takeout retries over "about two weeks", "it took me like a weekend", a 7.1k-star tool that exists only to repair Takeout imports, and a 2.5-year Immich thread demanding shared people that was closed only by a free release on 2026-10-07. |
| "Ownership that outlives any account" is itself a paid pain | weak | Vendors selling exactly this (Permanent.org $10/GB, FOREVER $180–$11,000 one-time) publish no user counts; GoodTrust now sells wills; only 12% of US adults set up free legacy controls. |

Net: the segment and the hours are real; the dollars are adjacent, not direct. That is a wedge-definition problem (sell into money that already moves — scanning, organizers, estates — and deliver the merge as the outcome) rather than a dead assumption. But as written in v0, "paid-for pain" is not demonstrated for the merge itself, and the "ownership outlives account" leg has the weakest demand evidence of all.

## Evidence AGAINST (sought first)

1. **The one product priced squarely on this pain shows no uptake.** Mylio Family: "Create a family library that connects generations"; "a shared photo library, where all family members can view and contribute to the family's history"; Starter $50/mo or $480/yr (guided setup for up to 5 members, "5 hours of personalized assistance yearly"); Plus $70/mo or $732/yr (up to 10 members, 2 TB SecureCloud, free yearly photo book); 2-week free onboarding, 30-day money-back [sourced: https://mylio.com/family/, ©2025, fetched 2026-11-03]. The page does not say whether members' libraries are merged or whether recognised people are shared across members; no launch date, no customer count; Mylio's blog has not posted since 2023-07-04 and its only mention of Mylio Family is a nav link [sourced: https://mylio.com/blog/, fetched 2026-11-03]; press page 404; homepage lists no user count [sourced: https://mylio.com/, fetched 2026-11-03]. Weight: moderate. A 12-year-old vendor with The Photo Managers as a channel partner (>700 members [sourced: einpresswire 2024-02, via competitive.md]) prices the job at $480–$732/yr and bundles human hours into it — consistent with a job that needs hand-holding and sells slowly, not with an acute mass-market pain. Mylio Family subscriber count: [unknown].
2. **A micro-vendor sells the full pitch for $49/yr and reports no customers.** KinPhotos: "Your scanned photos, in one gallery the family can search."; "Every family member can add their own."; "Click a face, type a name, done."; "It lasts as long as the family does."; "$49 a year" for unlimited members; free month up to 100 photos; "When the free month ends nothing is deleted and everyone can still browse." No user count, no founding date (© 2026 KinPhotos LLC) [sourced: https://kinphotos.com/, fetched 2026-11-03; /about 404]. Weight: weak-moderate — an existence proof that founders keep trying this, with no evidence anyone pays.
3. **The self-hosted community's demand for cross-user faces has just been met for free.** Immich v3.3.0 (2026-10-07): "people can now be shared!"; "Shows up in both users' list of people (on mobile and web)"; "Person sharing is directional."; "you can now merge a face from your own assets with people shared with you from other users" [sourced: https://github.com/immich-app/immich/releases/tag/v3.3.0, 2026-10-07]. Announcement thread: 24 upvotes, 141 comments; "Sharing 'John' means 'John' shows up in another user's list of people."; a user reports cross-account merge with "ZERO ISSUES!!!"; extension to partner sharing deferred ("time crunch!") [sourced: https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/32196, 2026-10-07]. Partner sharing itself still excludes "People and facial recognition data", is "one-way", and "duplicates are only detected on a per-user basis" [sourced: https://docs.immich.app/features/partner-sharing, fetched 2026-11-03]. Weight: moderate. The population most willing to spend hours on this (118k-star project [sourced: competitive.md]) now gets cross-user faces at $0; what remains unserved is cross-*account* (Apple↔Google) merge, which Immich users solve by Takeout + immich-go.
4. **Where this exact question is asked in public, engagement is low.** Apple Community "Unified photo library for all of family" (2022-05-14): 7 replies, **1** "Me too", closed; the top reply says Apple has been asked before and that it creates "serious and significant issues" around editing and file ownership; the "$150 per month for a max of 10 users" app is one user's unnamed find [sourced: https://discussions.apple.com/thread/253896284, 2022-05-14, fetched 2026-11-03]. "How to merge two iCloud Photo Libraries" (2025-11-19): 15 replies, no accepted solution, closed; tools offered: Apple's import-from-library, PowerPhotos ($40), Shared Library [sourced: https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256192786, 2025-11-19, fetched 2026-11-03]. "Ask HN: Archiving Family Photos?" (2026-05-25): **2 points, 3 comments**; the ask concerns one deceased relative's photos; the asker does not mention multiple relatives' libraries, dedupe, faces, or account ownership; answers are SSDs, M-DISC, FamilySearch, LTO [sourced: https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/48268326, 2026-05-25]. "Ask HN: Suggestions for archiving extended families photographs etc." (2020-05-26): 2 points, 1 comment [sourced: https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22family%20photos%22&tags=ask_hn, fetched 2026-11-03]. Weight: moderate for "acute": the *backup* question draws crowds (321 points / 375 comments, 2022-01-18; 230 / 121 for "2025 stack for a self-hosted photo library", 2025-06-30 [sourced: same search]); the *extended-family archive* question does not.
5. **In the big backup thread, families say shared storage is enough.** Of the first ~100k characters of the 2022 thread, only about 5–7 comments concern combining family members' libraries and "Most describe shared access or a common family store rather than true merging": "all important family members have accounts and can store all photos in that folder." (29978564); "We all have iPhones, so we use an app to automatically upload our pictures to the central nas." (29978848); "Then I share my Amazon Prime backup with my wife." (29980985); ~25 comments name a paid service (Backblaze, S3/Glacier, Google One, iCloud, pCloud, Prime Photos, SmugMug) — i.e. money goes to *storage*, not merge; ~8–10 mention time ("it took me like a weekend", "maybe an hr a month") [sourced: https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/items/29978099, 2022-01-18; first 100k chars only]. HN partner-sharing comments: two households say Google Partner Sharing works for them (24970052, 2020-11-02; 38309296, 2023-11-17); one calls it "the critical feature" (27339588, 2021-05-31); one (2026-09-21, 49790154) lists Immich's lack of cross-user facial recognition as a shortcoming [sourced: https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22partner%20sharing%22%20photos&tags=comment, fetched 2026-11-03].
6. **"Ownership that outlives any account" has sellers but no visible buyers.** Permanent.org: "Storage fees are only charged once: $10 per gigabyte of storage."; 1 GB free; Legacy Contact; "Backed by a mission-driven, cultural heritage not for profit organization" — no archive, member or file count anywhere on the homepage [sourced: https://www.permanent.org/, fetched 2026-11-03; /about/ and /annual-report/ 404; ProPublica nonprofit search returned "No nonprofits found" for "permanent legacy foundation"]. FOREVER: mission "where millions of families save and enjoy their memories" (aspiration, not a count); no member, file or employee figures; guarantee "your lifetime, plus 100 years"; ambassador (referral) sales model [sourced: https://www.forever.com/about, ©2026, fetched 2026-11-03]. GoodTrust — a 2020-era "digital legacy" startup — now sells "Wills and trusts" plus a Digital Vault as "Estate+" for "Only $149" and claims only "thousands of people" [sourced: https://mygoodtrust.com/, fetched 2026-11-03]. Artifact ($149 recorded family interviews) "is shutting down" [sourced: heyartifact.com via competitive.md]. ThisLife, the "aggregate all your sources" startup, was bought for ~$25M and folded into Shutterfly's print funnel [sourced: Wikipedia Shutterfly via competitive.md]. Pre-need behaviour: 12% of US adults have account-level legacy controls (T&W 2026, n=5,000) [sourced: customers.md]. Weight: moderate. Every vendor selling "outlives the account" is either tiny, silent on scale, or has pivoted to wills.
7. **The profession that does this by hand is small and slow.** The Photo Managers' own 2026 survey "of about 250 professional photo managers": "typical rates ranging from $50 to $150 per hour"; "many client projects required more than 20 hours of work"; "Most professionals served between one and three clients each month"; "more than half considered it their main profession"; services named are print organizing, scanning, digital organizing, photo books, home-movie conversion — consolidating across devices/accounts and inherited digital accounts are not named [sourced: https://thephotomanagers.com/how-to-start-a-photo-organizing-business/, 2026-08-25]. The association's Aug–Oct 2026 blog (12 posts) covers print boxes, estate cleanouts, downsizing, Canva photo books, cloud-storage costs, backup, AI — none announces a member survey of client demand, a member count, or multi-relative consolidation [sourced: https://thephotomanagers.com/blog/, fetched 2026-11-03]. The estate-cleanout post (2026-10-20) is about print boxes and says nothing about deceased relatives' digital accounts; the downsizing post (2026-09-15) resolves sibling disputes with "A quick round of phone snapshots" and names no tools, hours or costs [sourced: https://thephotomanagers.com/the-forgotten-photo-boxes-that-turn-up-during-estate-cleanouts-and-moves/, 2026-10-20; https://thephotomanagers.com/helping-aging-parents-downsize-without-losing-the-family-story/, 2026-09-15]. Channel size, illustrative: 700 members × 2 clients/month × 12 = 16,800 projects/yr; at 20 h × $50–$150/h = $1,000–$3,000 per project → **$17M–$50M/yr** of labour, all-in, across the whole association [estimated: 700 members (einpresswire 2024-02) × midpoint of "one and three clients each month" × 12 × "more than 20 hours" × survey rate range; upper-bound-ish because not all members are active practitioners, lower-bound-ish because projects exceed 20 h]. Weight: moderate. Real money, but a US pool in the low tens of millions — and it is paid for *labour on prints*, mostly.
8. **Dedupe is a commodity.** MacPaw Gemini: "Starting at $1.95/month", 4.5★ on 469 reviews [sourced: https://macpaw.com/gemini, fetched 2026-11-03]; PowerPhotos: "New License - $39.95", "All products are one time purchases, no subscriptions." [sourced: https://www.fatcatsoftware.com/store, fetched 2026-11-03]; Apple Photos has a built-in duplicate finder (described as "a little flaky") [sourced: discussions.apple.com/thread/256192786 via customers.md]. Nobody prices dedupe as a subscription for consumers.
9. **Scanning vendors sell digitization and then try — and fail to disclose — a cloud upsell.** Legacybox: "Every Legacybox includes a free 30-day Legacybox Membership trial"; "your access includes secure file storage on Fortune 100 servers"; post-trial price not stated on the FAQ or pricing page; membership page 404; help centre 404 [sourced: https://legacybox.com/pages/faq and https://legacybox.com/pages/pricing, fetched 2026-11-03]. Note the vendor's own scale claims conflict: "Trusted by 1.5 Million" (pricing page) vs "Over 2 million families" (homepage, per customers.md) [sourced: both pages, fetched 2026-11-03]. Heirloom Cloud: itemized per-media prices (photo scanning "From $29.99 (was $60.00)"), 90 days of cloud access then optional USB; 395 reviews [sourced: https://heirloom.cloud/, fetched 2026-11-03]. Weight: weak-moderate. The largest scanner in the category cannot or will not show a price for keeping the result in a shared cloud, which is where the "merge" would live.
10. **Inherited photos are a context problem, not a merge problem, in the one direct HN account.** "Inherited parents' albums and photos had no dates or labels, leaving them irrelevant to the next generation" (29981250, 2022-01-18) [sourced: https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22my%20parents%22%20%22google%20photos%22%20library&tags=comment, fetched 2026-11-03; 4 hits, 2 irrelevant]. HN comments mentioning PowerPhotos by name: 1 of 4 hits (others are Canon "PowerShot") and it does not mention merging or paying [sourced: https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=PowerPhotos&tags=comment, fetched 2026-11-03].

## Evidence FOR

1. **The pain recurs for years, unresolved, in primary threads.** Apple Community 2019-05-13 ("Data from one account can not be 'merged' into another account."), 2021-03-22, 2022-05-14, 2025-11-19 ("How can I get the rest of his photos from iCloud onto library B and then merge the two A & B so it's one library?"); HN 2022-01-18 ("Because Apple has't solved what everyone ask — a way for two or more users to merge Photos"); GitHub immich #14477 2024-12 → 2026-09 (Takeout omits other contributors' shared-album photos) [sourced: customers.md Q1(g) and Q4, URLs there]. Apple's own community doc "Notes on Merging Photos Libraries" (last modified 2019-03-03): "There is currently no completely lossless way to merge Photos Libraries."; iCloud merge "will not include the Faces albums"; export/re-import: "Your metadata will be gone"; PowerPhotos: "you will either lose the editing work or the high quality originals." [sourced: https://discussions.apple.com/docs/DOC-10211, 2019-03-03, fetched 2026-11-03]. Platforms confirm the gap: "Face labels are private to each account and aren't shared across accounts."; "Face groups aren't shared when you share photos." — no 2026 change on the page [sourced: https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128838, fetched 2026-11-03]; Google Photos' blog feed Oct 2025 → Oct 2026 (20 posts) contains nothing on shared libraries, account merge, cross-account faces or legacy [sourced: https://blog.google/products/photos/rss/, fetched 2026-11-03].
2. **Hours are demonstrably spent.** Takeout: one user "retried a Google Takeout export over about two weeks. Each attempt ran for hours before failing" (38068100, 2023-10-30); another's ~100 GB Apple export "took a few days" (37827183, 2023-10-10) [sourced: https://hn.algolia.com/api/v1/search?query=%22google%20takeout%22%20photos%20hours&tags=comment, fetched 2026-11-03]. immich-go — a tool whose purpose is to repair Takeout archives ("intelligently matches photos with their JSON metadata to preserve albums, descriptions, and locations"; "Avoids re-uploading files that already exist") — has 7.1k stars / 276 forks [sourced: https://github.com/simulot/immich-go, fetched 2026-11-03]. Immich "The definition of partner sharing" (2024-02-11, 68 votes, 60 comments): "people and face matching should be global state, not user state" (MikeBishop, 2024-02-24); "One single library with multiple contributors." (jhfry, 2025-04-23); maintainers: "We have plan for rework the whole permission mechanism" (2025-05-31) [sourced: https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions/7038, 2024-02 → 2025-05, fetched 2026-11-03]. "[Feature]: User Groups for sharing/permissions" (2022-12-02): 146 upvotes [sourced: https://github.com/immich-app/immich/discussions?discussions_q=is%3Aopen+merge+libraries+family, fetched 2026-11-03].
3. **Adjacent money is real and sized.** Scanning: Legacybox kits $69.98–$2,499.98, iMemories $0.99/print ($0.49 promo), EverPresent $0.68–$0.98/print + $50 fee, Photomyne revenue $17.01M in 2025 (+30%) and $59.99/yr or $19.99/mo membership with AI credits "purchased separately" [sourced: customers.md Q1(d); https://photomyne.com/pricing, fetched 2026-11-03]. Organizers: $50–$150/h, >20 h per project, 1–3 clients/month [sourced: thephotomanagers.com 2026-08-25]. Genealogy: Ancestry 3.8M+ subscribers at ≈$20–40/mo; MyHeritage sells a standalone Photo subscription [sourced: customers.md Q1(a)]. Family capture apps: Tinybeans ~95k paid subs at $75 ARPU; FamilyAlbum ¥17.1B segment, 12M MAU [sourced: customers.md Q1(h)]. Vendors betting on the multigenerational framing: Tinybeans+ Family (6 accounts, 1 TB, launching FY26) and "Multigenerational memory preservation" strategy; Tinybeans: organised/findable photos is "the number-one job that parents told us they would pay for" [sourced: customers.md Q1(h), ASX filings 2025-08-21 and 2026-04-28].
4. **Someone has priced the exact job high and attached human hours to it.** Mylio Family $480–$732/yr with guided setup and 5 h/yr of assistance; Mylio Concierge $299/$379 (three 1-hour sessions with a professional photo manager) [sourced: https://mylio.com/family/, fetched 2026-11-03; mylio.com/concierge via competitive.md]. This is evidence that the people closest to the customer (Mylio + The Photo Managers) believe the job is worth hundreds of dollars a year — and evidence that it currently requires a human in the loop.
5. **Life-event versions are acute and legally forced.** Apple: single-heir rule, court order in the US without a Legacy Contact; divorce: "Disconnect the account and you might lose access to years of images you assumed were 'yours.'"; 41,160 photos stuck between personal and Shared Library for months [sourced: customers.md Q1(b), Q1(f)].

## What observable fact would flip the verdict

To **holds** (any one, primary source):
- A published paying-customer count or revenue for a merge-specific product: Mylio Family subscribers, KinPhotos paying families, or Tinybeans+ Family (6-account tier) subscribers broken out in an ASX filing, at a level that implies ≥ low thousands of households paying ≥ $100/yr for the consolidated-family-library job.
- A survey with named methodology (n ≥ 1,000, nat-rep) showing a double-digit share of households that have *paid* (not "would pay") to consolidate multiple relatives' photo collections, or ≥ 10 hours spent on it in the last 12 months.
- The Photo Managers publishing member-survey data in which "consolidating libraries from multiple family members/accounts" is a top-3 requested service.
- A pre-sale test by this venture: ≥ 30 of ~100 interviewed buyers in a named segment (e.g. executor/heir, divorce, downsizing) paying a deposit ≥ $99 before the product exists.

To **broken** (any one):
- Apple or Google ships cross-account library merge with shared people/faces (would also break A4).
- Mylio Family withdrawn or re-priced below ~$100/yr within 12 months, or Mylio Concierge discontinued — i.e. the vendor closest to the job concludes it will not sell.
- A landing-page/pre-sale test for the merge product converting below ~1% of qualified traffic, or interview evidence that heirs/organizers' clients prefer a one-time human service and will not subscribe.
- Immich/Ente shipping cross-*account* (Google/Apple import) merge with shared faces for free, removing the last unserved layer for the hours-spending segment.

Would raise confidence in "partial" toward 0.8: Tinybeans' Oct 2026 4C and FY26 annual report (gated at ASX, see below) showing the Family tier's uptake either way; Legacybox's membership price and retention.
