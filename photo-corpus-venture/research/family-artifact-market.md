# Track R13 — Paid family-photo artifacts for grandparents: Wi-Fi frames and printed journals

**Bottom line (five lines; rewritten 2026-11-25 after the round-3 audit — see the Audit log at the end).**
1. Frames: adult children do pay once for a grandparent artifact, but the largest *filed* frame revenue is eight figures and shrinking — Nixplay $57.9M FY2021 → $20.0M FY2023 [sourced: S13; S14]. The only nine-figure numbers are Skylight's unaudited company claims — "$300M+" (hiring listing, undated; logged by a tracker on 2026-05-06) and "more than $500 million" (president quoted, 2026-07-31) [sourced: S9; sourced-secondary: S67, S10] — and they cover Frame + Calendar + Buddy together; the Frame/Calendar revenue split is [unknown]. Aura revenue is [unknown].
2. Printed family journals are a proven eight-figure category in one country: Famileo, €15.3M in 2025, eleven years after its 2014 founding [sourced: S24], growing between +2% and +9% that year depending on which 2024 base is used, with a +8% target for 2026 [sourced: S25a; estimated: 15.3 ÷ 15 and 15.3 ÷ 14.0; 16.5 ÷ 15.3]. No journal operator anywhere has a sourced nine-figure revenue. Neveo's last sourced figure is €2M for 2023; 2024–2026 [unknown] [sourced: S26].
3. US journal traction is thin, not zero, and is measured only by proxy: Famileo's US iOS app has 110 ratings against 5,322 in France [sourced: S5]; Famileo Pro names US senior-living customers [sourced: S23]; US start-ups report "520+" and "1,000+" families [sourced: S48, S49]. No operator publishes a US subscriber count [unknown].
4. Automatic photo selection is table stakes (Recently since 2015, Google 2020, Chatbooks, FamilyAlbum's AI monthly book at $7.99) [sourced: S35, S40, S56, S57, S65], the two journals that scaled are deliberately manual [sourced: S21, S27], and frames from about $59 (generic, estimator price) to $149 (Aura Carver list) with no subscription already do the "photos to grandma" job [sourced-secondary: S17; sourced: S4].
5. Verdict (direction unchanged by the repair): the evidence does **not** support a nine-figure outcome for a printed-journal venture. Sourced journal revenues span €2M (Neveo, 2023) to €15.3M (Famileo, 2025) [estimated: range of the two sourced operators, S26 and S25a]. US gross margin is [unknown]: at public print-on-demand prices plus First-Class postage a 20-page US journal costs $9.24–10.09 against a $9.99 price [estimated: section Q3], so the business exists only with contract print pricing nobody has quoted us.

Date of research: 2026-11-24; repaired 2026-11-25. URLs accessed 2026-11-24 unless a source entry says 2026-11-25. Currency as printed (€, $, £); no FX applied, so no cross-currency ratios are stated. Estimator sites (Growjo, Latka, Owler, RocketReach, ZoomInfo, Grips, ARR Club, Asinsight, Simplify, Kingscrowd) are labelled wherever used; where a conclusion leans on one, that is said on the line.

## 1. Questions asked

(1) Aura, Skylight, Nixplay: revenue, units, funding, prices, subscriptions, storage policy, gift share, ratings, seasonality, 2025–26 products, retail. What do they prove about adult children paying? (2) Printed journals: Famileo, Neveo, Recently, Chatbooks, Lalo, Kindred, Tiny Prints, Timeshel, Groovebook — facts and outcomes, dead and alive. (3) Mechanics and unit economics: price vs print + postage; margin, churn, how photos are chosen. (4) Auto-assembly: who already does it, what reviews say, differentiator or table stakes. (5) US demand evidence: device use, print preference, spend, senior-living programmes, US traction of Famileo-style products. (6) Platform and incumbent risk: Shutterfly/Snapfish/FreePrints, FamilyAlbum prints, Amazon, Apple, Google. (7) Falsification: do recurring-print subscriptions churn or die; have frames commoditised the job.

### Q3. Mechanics and unit economics

- **How photos get in.** Famileo and Neveo are manual: each relative posts a "message" (photo plus caption) in the app; the plan is metered in messages (20/28/36) or photos (30) [sourced: S21, S27]. Layout and print are automatic ("se fabrique presque tout seul") [sourced: S21]. Nothing is pulled from the camera roll.
- **US mail class (resolved 2026-11-25).** A per-family journal must travel as First-Class Mail: "Mail containing personal information must be mailed as First-Class Mail… Personal information is any information specific to the addressee" [sourced: S64, DMM 233.2.3]; USPS Marketing Mail requires that "The contents of printed matter… must be identical to a piece sent to at least one other addressee" and excludes personal information except in support of advertising [sourced: S64, DMM 243.3.2.10, 243.2.2]. Marketing Mail flats ($0.783–$1.299 per piece at 4 oz or less, commercial automation/non-automation [sourced: S44]) are therefore not available. Periodicals eligibility [unknown — DMM 207 not read; a publication whose every copy differs and consists of personal content is unlikely to qualify, inference].
- **US postage (Notice 123, effective 2026-10-04) [sourced: S44].** First-Class large envelope (flat), retail single-piece: 1 oz $1.69, 2 oz $1.98, 3 oz $2.27, 4 oz $2.56; stamped letter 1 oz $0.82. First-Class Mail *commercial* flats: 3 oz $1.605 (automation 5-digit) / $1.844 (3-digit/SCF) / $2.165 (mixed) / $2.170 (machinable presorted); 4 oz $1.895 / $2.134 / $2.455 / $2.460. Presorted First-Class mailings need at least 500 pieces per mailing [sourced: S64, DMM 233.3.3 — the sentence read is the letters standard; assumed the same for flats]. A 20-page 8.5×11 booklet is five 11×17 sheets ≈ 71–89 g ≈ 2.5–3.2 oz before the envelope [estimated: 5 × 0.1206 m² × 118–148 gsm], so the 3 oz or 4 oz step applies. Journals addressed to scattered households will mostly earn the 3-digit or mixed rate, so a realistic commercial postage range is $1.84–2.46 [estimated: 3-digit 3 oz to presorted 4 oz from the table above], a saving of 4–19% on retail [estimated: 2.46 ÷ 2.56; 1.84 ÷ 2.27].
- **US print cost, public price lists.** Blurb magazine, 8.5×11: "$7.00 for one copy of a 20-page magazine", $0.20 per extra page, volume discounts from 10 copies [sourced: S43]. Lulu magazine, US Letter, 20 interior pages, saddle stitch, premium colour, 70# coated, glossy cover: $8.98 per copy; $8.53 at 100–499; $8.08 at 500–999; $7.63 at 1,000+ [sourced: S63, Lulu pricing calculator operated in a browser, 2026-11-25]. Mixam: "$5–$8" per PrintLink saddle-stitch copy, formerly "$5 for 32 to 48 pages" [sourced-secondary: S45]; its own calculator could not be read (prices load over a websocket; the browser session received HTTP 403) [unknown]. One undated roundup quotes Mixam at $620.40 per 1,000 8.5×11 booklets, spec unstated [sourced-secondary: S72b, search summary; page returned 429] — that is a static run of identical copies and does not apply to a journal where every copy is a run of one. Gelato (Cloudflare challenge), Peecho (pricing page gone), Cloudprinter (not attempted): [unknown]. Famileo's and Neveo's per-copy cost [unknown].
- **Price vs cost.** At public prices a 20-page US journal costs $9.24–10.09 to print and post at volume [estimated: Lulu $7.63 (1,000+) + commercial First-Class flat $1.605–2.460], or $9.27–9.56 as a one-off [estimated: Blurb $7.00 + retail flat $2.27–2.56], against Famileo's US price of $9.99 [sourced: S21]: gross margin between −1% and +8% before card fees, envelope and support [estimated: (9.99 − 10.09) ÷ 9.99; (9.99 − 9.24) ÷ 9.99]. *Withdrawn:* the earlier "print $2.50 → COGS $5.37 → gross margin 46%" scenario; the $2.50 had no source. No public quote comes within a factor of two of that. **US gross margin: [unknown] until a contract variable-data print quote exists.**

### Q7. Falsification

- **"Recurring print dies or churns fast."** Partly supported, weaker than first written. Dead: Groovebook (2022) [sourced-secondary: S32]; Kindred Prints [sourced: S37]; Google's first trial after four months [sourced: S41]. Alive but small by proxy: Timeshel, 308 iOS ratings twelve years after launch [sourced: S5; sourced-secondary: S36]; Recently, still a monthly membership after eleven years, 1,224 ratings [sourced: S65, S5] (the earlier "left the monthly magazine" is withdrawn). Chatbooks: one unverified portfolio page reports 150K cancellations in 2025 with no subscriber base [sourced-secondary: S30] — an anecdote, not a churn rate. Counter-evidence: Famileo's revenue rose at every sourced point — €3.5M (2020), >€13M (2023), ~€14M (2024), €15.3M (2025) [sourced: S24, S18, S25a] — and its family count rose from 160,000 (Feb 2021) to 280,000 (Nov 2026) with a flat stretch through 2025 [sourced: S25b, S20, S18, S21]; Neveo reported profitability from end-2021 [sourced: S26]. (The earlier "grown every year for ten years" had no source and is withdrawn.) Both mail to a **third party**; "gifts survive, self-purchases churn" is our reading, and it is **argued, not evidenced** — no retention or churn figure exists for any gift journal [unknown].
- **"Frames have commoditised the job at a one-time price."** Supported on installed-base evidence, indicative on price. Frameo's app has 16× Aura's iOS ratings and a 5M+ Play download band against 1M+ [sourced: S5, S6; estimated: 458,172 ÷ 27,980]; Aura and Skylight advertise "No Subscription" in product titles [sourced-secondary: S17]; Nixplay's attempt to add a fee produced ~1.2% paid [estimated: Q1, on inputs not re-verified] and a lawsuit [sourced: S16]. The $59.48 price and unit-rank leg comes from one estimator, one non-holiday month [sourced-secondary: S17] and should not carry weight alone. Residual room for paper: 21% of 65+ do not own a smartphone [estimated: 100 − 79, S47] (irrelevant to a frame, which needs only Wi-Fi and a relative to set it up), care-home residents, and homes without Wi-Fi [unknown share].
- **"Adult children pay for grandparent artifacts at scale."** Supported for one-time hardware [sourced: S13, S3]; supported for a €5.99 journal in France and Belgium [sourced: S19, S25a]; not evidenced either way for a $10–17 journal in the US [sourced: S21, S48 for the price range; traction unknown].
- **"A journal can reach nine figures."** Not supported. Scenario only: French-level *paying* penetration in the US at US prices ≈ 1.1M families × $119.88/yr ≈ $132M [estimated: 220,000 B2C families (S19) × 5 × $9.99 × 12 (S21); the 5× US/France population multiple is a round assumption, not sourced this round]. Against it: eleven years and a care-home seed channel to reach 280,000 families in France [sourced: S21, S25b]; revenue growth of +2% to +9% in 2025 [estimated: Q2]; a US/France rating ratio of 2% [estimated: 110 ÷ 5,322, S5; proxy]; and a US cost stack that leaves −1% to +8% gross margin at public print prices [estimated: Q3].
