## Stance

Default is no. The questions are who pays, how much, why now, through what channel, and what must be true for a $1B outcome. This variant is unusually candid: it says itself that "$100M does not pencil" and that it "survives as a family product that communities distribute, not as a line item operators defend." I take both statements at face value and score the business as specified, where the operator is the payer.

Verdict: pass. The variant swaps a 7,800-person profession for 56,207 facilities and lands lower, not higher, once the repaired research is applied.

## Ceiling, recomputed

Rule applied: use the research-supported end of each input; an untagged or contradicted input cannot carry the central case.

**Pool.** 2,256,424 residents in assisted living and nursing homes [institutional-legacy.md Q8]. Supported as a count. Overstated as a market: 43% of assisted-living communities are 4–10 unit owner-operated houses, where the variant's $3,000 minimum is $300–$750 per resident; and 1.24M of the residents (55%) are in nursing homes, where the only evidenced payer for life-story work is penalty-fund grants [institutional-legacy.md Q1, Q3].

**Penetration.** The variant's three points are an assumption (2%), a search-extract cumulative count from a competitor with $52M raised (5.5%), and a self-claim the research calls overstated (8.4%). The only dedicated life-story vendor with a sourced count is LifeBio: "more than 120 organizations" after 20 years [institutional-legacy.md Q3]. I keep the variant's points but note that nothing supports a seed-stage point solution reaching the upper two.

**Price.** $108 is a design choice. Its comparables are 2017 list prices from one trade article and a range the research has withdrawn. Current sourced prices: LifeBio $9–$18 per person per year [institutional-legacy.md Q3]; Famileo Pro US from $99 a month for up to 50 beds, which is about $24 a bed at 50 and $48 at 25 [family-artifact-market.md Q2]. Higher prices were paid only by grants ($306 per resident from penalty funds) or by nobody for long ($450 per story, vendor now a redirect stub).

## Most damaging objection

The repaired research found the 2026 market price for this exact deliverable, and it is below the variant's own unit cost.

LifeBio already sells "a single conversation … generates a digital care file overnight — powered by AI" plus a family book order (the variant's Why now 5). Its current group licence is $149 for a census of 100–199 people, read as monthly: about $9–$18 per person per year [institutional-legacy.md bottom line 5, Q3]. If that $149 is annual rather than monthly the price is twelve times lower again, so $9–$18 is the generous reading. The variant's central COGS is $1,260 for a 60-unit community, which is $21 per occupied unit per year, and $12–$38 across its own range. A 60-unit community at LifeBio's price yields $540–$1,080 a year against $1,260 of cost: gross margin between −133% and −17%. At Famileo Pro's US list price ($1,188 a year for up to 50 beds) it is about −6%; the price above 50 beds is not published, and if it doubled to $2,376 the margin would be 47%.

So the plan needs operators to pay 6–12 times the current list price of a 26-year-old incumbent whose product development is funded by $5.37M of NIH grants, while LifeLoop ships "Life Story" inside contracts it already holds, and to reach those operators one at a time through a 9–11 month sale worth $3,000–$6,480 a year. The variant's 81% gross margin and 19-month payback are both true only at $108. No operator is shown paying $108 from its own budget for this.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2)**, tied on score with Distribution (2). Ceiling is the one round 3 existed to fix, and the variant states it does not fix it. After the repair the gap is wider than the variant thought: its central case drops from $17M to about $4M.

Distribution deserves the same alarm. On the variant's own assumptions one seller costing $250,000 a year adds $213,000 of ARR. That is the best case, before churn, before the 9–11 month cycle, and with pilot-to-rollout conversion unknown [institutional-legacy.md Q5].

## Strongest dimension

**Buildability (6).** Nothing here waits on Apple, Google or a research result. The voice stack is a priced commodity [memoir-gift-market.md Q5], voice-to-tag over photos exists as prior art [memoir-gift-market.md Q4], and the first deliverable (the one-page summary) is built from family input before any resident interview. The consent design is more careful than most decks. This is a strength of the team's product thinking, not of the business: what is easy to build here was also easy for Porchlight's solo founder, by the variant's own account.

## Unknowns research could resolve before the next round

1. What do LifeLoop and TSOLife charge per community or per resident per year, and is LifeLoop "Life Story" bundled or a paid add-on? Search public procurement: county nursing homes, state veterans' homes, board minutes, CMP grant applications that attach vendor quotes.
2. Is LifeBio's "$149.00 (Monthly Billing)" licence for a 100–199 census charged per month or per year, how many organisations hold one in 2026, and what share of LifeBio's reported $2M was NIH or penalty-fund income?
3. Which real-time voice APIs (OpenAI realtime and GPT-Live, Gemini Live, Deepgram, ElevenLabs, Vapi, Retell, Twilio) will sign a HIPAA business associate agreement in 2026, on which plan, and with what minimum spend?
4. How many state civil-money-penalty reinvestment programmes other than Tennessee's have funded life-story or resident-engagement technology since 2018, and at what dollars per home? This tests whether grants are a repeatable payer or a one-state accident.
5. How many US communities use Famileo Pro today, against 200 UK and US establishments combined in February 2021, and what is its US renewal rate? It is the closest live test of this channel at $99 a month.

## Three questions for the founder

1. Name one operator that has paid $100 or more per resident per year, from its own operating budget and outside a pilot or grant, for a life-story programme. If you cannot, why is $108 your list price when LifeBio lists at $9–$18?
2. The day LifeLoop adds "family uploads photos, AI asks about them" to Life Story at no charge, what do you sell to a LifeLoop community, and who signs for it?
3. You wrote that this "survives as a family product that communities distribute." If so, the operator is a channel, not a customer. What is your runway against a 9–11 month first sale, which operators can you call today, and why is this not simply the gift variant with a slower and costlier acquisition path?
