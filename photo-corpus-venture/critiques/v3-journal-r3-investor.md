## Stance

Default is no. I score who pays, how much, through what channel, and what has to be true for $1B+. I do not credit hardware revenue as a comparable for a print subscription, and I do not credit a French outcome as a US forecast. Ceiling is recomputed from the research-supported end of each input. Where the variant (written 2026-11-24) disagrees with research repaired on 2026-11-25, the research wins.

One thing in the variant's favour before the scores: it says up front that $100M does not pencil and lands at $4–16M. That honesty is why this is a short memo and not a long one. It does not change the answer.

Arithmetic:
- Three times today's whole US category: 22,500 × $98 to $130 = **$2.2–2.9M**.
- 100,000 families (five times Neveo's last disclosed base): **$9.8–13.0M**. No US evidence supports it.

At a 35–50% gross margin, $13M of ARR is $4.5–6.5M of gross profit. That is a small business, not a venture outcome.

## Most damaging objection

**The category leader already sells this product in the US, at or below the variant's price, and has almost nothing to show for it.** Famileo is eleven years old, makes €15.3M, has had a US presence since at least early 2021, a US consumer page at $9.99–13.99, a US phone number and named senior-living customers. Its US iOS app has 110 ratings against 5,322 in France. Add Neveo (94 US ratings, whose founders named the US as their priority after France), Hug Letters (1,000+ families after a TODAY Show placement) and The Family Press (520+), and the whole US category is roughly 7,500 families and $1M a year.

The variant's answer is that selecting photos is too much work and on-device proposal fixes it. The research says otherwise: Famileo names paper and postage as its constraint, Neveo names acquisition cost, and both scaled while deliberately manual. So the differentiator attacks a problem no operator reports. It also depends on a full-library permission Apple's guideline steers away from, and FamilyAlbum (about 6M North American registrants, 380K US ratings, an AI-built monthly book with grandparent copies) can ship it in a release.

I would be funding the fifth sub-scale US entrant, with a feature aimed at the wrong bottleneck, a gross margin nobody has quoted, and no churn figure from any gift journal anywhere.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2).** About $1–3M on US evidence, $13M as an unsupported stretch, under $30M at the largest result the category has produced in any country. Round 3 was meant to fix Ceiling by selling an artifact people pay for. People do pay, and the artifact still caps at the same level as the organizer workbench.

## Strongest dimension

**Wedge (6).** This is the first variant whose first use needs no migration, no library upload and no action from the elder, and whose core habit is evidenced at six-figure scale: 220,000+ paying families, about seven contributors each, 2.5M gazettes mailed in 2025. The evidence is French and the behaviour is manual posting, not auto-proposal, so the credit goes to the journal format and not to this team's addition to it.

## Unknowns research could resolve before the next round

1. What do US digital printers quote for a variable-data (every copy unique) 24-page 5.5×8.5 saddle-stitched booklet at 1,000, 10,000 and 50,000 copies a month, and does USPS classify a 1.8 oz enveloped booklet as an automation-compatible First-Class letter or as a flat?
2. What do the statutory accounts of Entourage Solutions (Famileo; Pappers, Infogreffe or INPI) and Neveo SA (National Bank of Belgium filings) show for gross margin, marketing spend and operating profit in 2023–2025, and does either disclose subscriber churn or average subscription length?
3. How many US subscribers do Famileo, Neveo, Hug Letters, The Family Press, Lineage, FamilyPost and NanaGram have, by third-party app-intelligence estimates of US downloads and revenue and by the Meta Ad Library record of how long each has run US ads?
4. What is the annual probability that a US grandparent aged 75–90 dies or moves into assisted living or a nursing home (SSA period life table plus NCAL and KFF admission data), which sets the churn floor for this product?
5. What share of iOS users grant full rather than limited photo access to print apps that request it (Recently, Chatbooks, FreePrints, Popsa), and has App Review rejected any auto-curation app under Guideline 5.1.1(iii)?

## Three questions for the founder

1. Famileo has sold this in the US since 2021 at your price and has about a hundred App Store ratings. What do you believe is stopping it here, and is that something auto-proposal fixes, or is it price, awareness and a $60 frame?
2. On the evidence this is a $3–13M print-and-mail business at a 35–50% gross margin. Do you want to run that, on what runway, and is it a venture-funded company or a bootstrapped one?
3. When the recipient dies, or moves into a home where Famileo Pro is free to the family, what does the family pay you for next, and what pilot result would make you drop the "archive as retention asset" claim?
