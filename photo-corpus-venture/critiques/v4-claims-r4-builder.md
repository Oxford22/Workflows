## What breaks at 10,000 customers

- **There are not 10,000 customers.** 10,000 paying households a year is 36-73% of every fire-segment household still asked to itemize (25,000-50,000 x about 0.55), nationally, beside two free tools. At the variant's 10% paid share it implies 100,000 free users, two to four times the whole fire segment.
- **Demand arrives as one region in one month.** Support for people who have just lost a home cannot be staffed for a spike and then idled; the $25 support line is an assumption with no source.
- **Free users are the cost.** At 9 free to 1 paid the variant's own margin is 28%. Stopping the free tier before item listing makes it worse than Bevel, which is free end to end.
- **Store commission is not in the table.** The $6.07 fee assumes a card payment on the web. If the $199 unlock is sold inside the iOS app, the sourced rate is 15% ($29.85), central cost becomes about $77 and margin about 61%. My recomputation.
- **Third parties' photos go to a server and to a third-party model unblurred.** Blurring happens only in the export. No face template is computed, which keeps this outside the fact patterns in face-regulation.md Q1, but App Store 5.1.2(i) requires explicit permission before sharing personal data with third-party AI (memory-product-sdk.md Q6), and whether on-device face detection alone is a biometric issue is [unknown] in research, as the variant says.
- **Data-loss liability.** For up to 36 months the company holds the only organised evidence for a six-figure claim. Losing it, or deleting it on schedule while a dispute is open, is the company's problem. Bevel advertises SOC 2 Type II; the capital plan has no line for it.
- **Fraud and records exposure.** A tool that lists every background object will be named in a padded claim; §10103.7(b)(6) preserves the insurer's right to restrict payment on suspected fraud. 16% of Los Angeles respondents hired an attorney, so the company should expect requests for its records in disputes. No research on that cost; my inference.
- **Licensing.** Selling priced, export-ready claim documents for a fee sits near California's "aids in any manner"; no enforcement found, no regulator has opined, states beyond three not read.

## Most damaging objection

The four weeks build the part that is already free and skip the part that is unproven. Photo-to-item-list ships today at $0 from a team with $6M (Bevel) and from a public-adjuster firm (Loti), and three hackathon teams reproduced it. What this variant adds is three things, and each fails on inspection: (1) the on-device address search is something a survivor can do in Apple Photos by typing the address, and it disappears when the user grants limited access or the relative is on Android; (2) the relative invite is a share link Bevel can add; (3) the per-line "dated, hash-stamped evidence" cannot prove what it claims, because the app hashes a downscaled copy after the loss and reads a capture date the user can edit. So the product is a free competitor's upload form plus a metadata filter, priced at $199 with no observed buyer at any stand-alone price, in a segment the repaired research sizes at $1.2M-$2.3M a year at the only adoption rate ever observed.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (1).** The research's reading is low single-digit millions at any observed price and adoption. The variant's own $1.5M central is built on a pool, an itemizing share and a carrier price that the repaired research withdrew; rebuilt, its household line is about $0.2M central. Nine figures exists only as a licensed adjusting firm taking most of the segment, which is a services company under 10% caps in Texas, Florida emergencies and Utah catastrophes.

## Strongest dimension

**Pain (6).** It is the first variant in which a named dollar amount depends on the output: after California's 60% advance, 40% of the contents limit is released only against a list, which is $100,000 on a $500,000 dwelling at the default ratio (research's illustration). Two surveys agree that itemization is the most common difficulty, more than half of respondents were still told to list every item a year on, and 15.9% hired a public adjuster and 16.1% an attorney, both paid on contingency (overlap between the two not reported). It stays at 6 because the event is roughly a 0.1%-a-year occurrence per household and nobody was found paying for the inventory itself.

## Unknowns research could resolve before the next round

1. What share of photos in a typical iPhone library carry a stored location, and does it survive for photos saved from Messages, WhatsApp and AirDrop? (Search: published measurements of EXIF GPS presence in consumer photo libraries; Apple documentation on location metadata in shared photos.)
2. What share of users choose full versus limited access at the iOS photo-library prompt? (Search: developer-reported opt-in rates for PHAuthorizationStatus limited versus authorized, 2023-2026.)
3. Has any insurer, state insurance department or court stated what pre-loss photographs prove in a contents claim, or rejected photo-derived lines? (Search: carrier claim manuals and department bulletins on proof of ownership for personal property; "pre-loss photos" contents claim denial.)
4. As of November 2026, does Bevel or Loti ship multi-contributor invites, cloud-library import or per-item evidence dates, and what does Loti's $100-a-month suite convert at? (Search: Bevel and Loti product pages, changelogs, app listings, press.)
5. Is there a published accuracy figure for matching the same household object across casual photos taken years apart, or for duplicate detection in any contents-inventory product? (Search: object instance re-identification benchmarks for indoor consumer photos; XactContents "multi-object recognition" and Ashline duplicate-checking documentation.)

## Three questions for the founder

1. Do you, or someone you would bring in, hold a public-adjuster licence or have worked contents claims? The research shows the only route above low single-digit millions is a licensed contingency firm. Is that a company you would run?
2. Can you get five families, ideally Los Angeles survivors who have already finished an inventory, to export the photos taken at their address this week, so the recall question is answered with a notebook before anyone writes an app?
3. If the recall test passes, would you ship this as a free "claim evidence pack" inside the organizer workbench with no revenue line of its own, or do you need this variant to be the company? The evidence supports the first and not the second.
