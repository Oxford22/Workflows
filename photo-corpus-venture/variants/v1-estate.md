## One line

When someone dies their photos come out of the cloud exactly once — through Apple's Legacy Contact download, a court order, a Google deceased-user request, or the unlocked phone in a drawer — and the heirs have days to produce a tribute and years to settle the rest; we are the guided path for that export and the place it lands: deduplicated, with the deceased and the family named, jointly owned by the heirs, with a tribute-ready selection on day one and an optional ongoing vault and pre-need plan for the relatives who just watched it happen.

## Wedge (first use case adopted without changing habits; the first 10 minutes; day-1 value for ONE user; day-7 value)

**Why this needs no migration.** The event itself produces the export: Apple hands the Legacy Contact a bulk download (14-day window, originals) [sourced: support.apple.com/102208, 2026-09-11]; a court order ends in the same iCloud.com/privacy.apple.com download; the heir with the passcode already has to get the photos off a phone that will be erased or sold; Google's processes end in a Takeout-style archive. None of these is a behavior change — they are the only way the heir gets the photos at all, and the alternative is loss on Apple's 3-year or Google's 2-year clock. The round-0 killer (unattended iOS upload, A2b "broken in practice") does not apply: the decedent's phone is not being asked to run anything; the heir's Mac, browser or our ingest endpoint receives files the platform already produced.

**First use case: the tribute pack, before any export exists.** The thing with a date is the service. The heir does not yet have Dad's library on day 2; they have their own phone, their siblings' phones, and a few shared albums. First use: "photos of Dad, from everything we already hold, deduplicated, dated, in the order and format the funeral home wants." That is delivered from the living relatives' own libraries — indexed on their own devices with no migration (the v1-federation mechanic: MobileCLIP + Vision faces, ~15 min for 30k photos on-device) [estimated: research/ios-feasibility.md Q3 Scenario A] — plus any folders or Shared Albums they drag in on the web.

**The first 10 minutes (one heir, web + iPhone).**
1. Triage page: "Who died? What do you have?" — four buttons: the phone and passcode; a Legacy Contact access key; neither; a Google account. Each leads to a step-by-step for that path (Apple's own articles, re-sequenced; the court-order path is a checklist and a state list, not legal advice).
2. "Start the tribute now": install the iPhone app or use the Mac app; tap three photos of the deceased; the app indexes the heir's own library on-device and surfaces the cluster; the heir confirms. Nothing of the heir's library is uploaded beyond ~256 px previews and vectors for the selected cluster [estimated: unit-economics.md Q7 index-only row assumptions].
3. Invite siblings by link; each repeats step 2 on their own phone. Cross-library duplicates collapse; a timeline forms.
4. Export the tribute pack: 30–100 selections as JPEG, chronologically numbered, in the format funeral slideshow tools accept, plus a shareable link for the funeral director. Free.

**Day-1 value for ONE user.** A single heir with only their own iPhone gets every photo they hold of the deceased found, deduplicated and dated, exported in a funeral-ready folder, in under an hour — a job Siri AI/Ask Photos partly do for one library (search) but do not finish (dedupe across relatives, export pack, a date-ordered set). Honest: for a single heir with a small library this is modest and the price is $0; its job is to be the first touch that makes the paid step natural.

**Day-7 value.** The service is over; the aftercare text arrives (Tukios: sympathy text two weeks after the service, review request at six weeks) [sourced: https://www.tukios.com/aftercare, fetched 2026-11-03]. Dad's export has landed or the path to it is clear. The Recovery Kit ingests whatever arrived — the privacy.apple.com zip, iCloud.com batches, a Mac Photos library (via the Photos.sqlite reader: assets, albums, edit/original pairs, face regions and person names) [sourced: osxphotos API/CLI via platform-access.md Q3], Takeout zips with sidecar repair, Image Capture pulls from the unlocked phone — dedupes it against what the family already assembled, carries Apple's existing People names across where the Mac path is used, and asks the family to confirm identities. Output: one archive the heirs co-own (each gets a full export; names as XMP `PersonInImage` + MWG regions; Apple Photos re-imports names only as keywords) [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q9], the deceased's phone can be erased, and the Apple 3-year clock stops mattering.

**The no-access case (most decedents), what the product still does.** On the 4–12% proxy, most heirs have neither a Legacy Contact nor (for many) the passcode. The product still (a) builds the deceased's archive from the living relatives' phones and shared albums (steps 2–3), which is where most photos *of* an older decedent live anyway (share [unknown]); (b) hands the executor the court-order checklist and the one documented outcome (~10,000 items recovered) so they can decide whether $750–1,200 is worth it; (c) files the Google deceased-user request with them; (d) sets Legacy Contact / IAM for every living relative who just learned what happens without it. What it cannot do: unlock a phone, or recover anything Apple cannot decrypt.

## Product (core loop; explicitly NOT in v1)

**Core loop (per estate).**
1. **Triage and guided path**: four paths (Legacy Contact; court order; phone + passcode; Google), each a sequenced checklist built on the platform's own articles, with the clocks shown (Apple 3 years from approval; privacy.apple.com 14-day download; Google 2-year inactivity) [sourced above]. Concierge option: a certified photo manager does the export with the heir (TPM: "hundreds" certified; Mylio Concierge precedent: three 1-hour sessions for $299) [sourced: pro-channel.md Q1, Q3].
2. **Collect**: relatives' on-device indexes (iOS/Mac; previews + vectors only); web uploads of folders/zips; Mac app ingest of a `.photoslibrary`, privacy.apple.com export, iCloud.com batches, Takeout (sidecar repair), Image Capture from an unlocked iPhone; scanner output later.
3. **Dedupe + repair**: exact → perceptual → edit/original and resolution-variant pairing; EXIF/XMP normalisation; a per-item provenance ledger (every input → one output decision).
4. **Assisted identity**: the deceased is seeded by the heir and named first (no consent gate); living relatives are anonymous clusters until they accept an invite and tick a separate biometric-consent screen, or are marked deceased [sourced: face-regulation.md Q7 architecture]; cross-decade child→adult matching is suggestion-only (child→adult across decades is an open research problem; adult↔aged-adult ~98% AgeDB-30) [sourced: ai-why-now.md Q3].
5. **Outputs**: (a) tribute pack (free); (b) the family archive — co-owned, each heir can export everything (folder tree + XMP, Apple Photos import with albums + name keywords, Google upload via `appendonly`) [sourced: platform-access.md Q4; ios-feasibility.md Q9]; (c) optional vault: index + previews + notes hosted, originals optional and cold-tiered, MCP endpoint with a Claude install link [sourced: research/assumptions/A6.md For #1]; (d) pre-need plan for the living: Legacy Contact / IAM set-up walkthrough, a "who gets the archive" designation, annual reminder.
6. **Channel surface**: a white-label page a funeral home or hospice can link from its aftercare text; a dashboard showing which families started (no photo content visible to the home).

**Explicitly NOT in v1.** Unlocking or forensically recovering locked phones; legal services (we provide checklists and point to counsel; no petitions drafted); migration apps for the living (no iOS background upload, no osxphotos-for-consumers); automatic cross-generation face matching; server-side face matching for living people in IL/TX/WA/EU [sourced: face-regulation.md Q7 item 5]; storing originals by default; scraping obituaries or Legacy.com for leads (see Distribution); Android on-device indexing (index yes later; the Prompt API is flagship-only and foreground-only) [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q7].

**Sensitivity and ethics of selling at grief (the critics will ask).**
- **Two moments, two offers.** The 1–3-day scramble gets a free product with no account required; the paid kit is offered only after the service, through the family's own funeral home, hospice or employer programme, at the two-week and six-week touchpoints those programmes already use [sourced: tukios.com/aftercare; 42 CFR 418.64(d)]. No cold outreach, no obituary scraping (Legacy.com's 30M monthly uniques are not a lead list for us) [sourced: estate.md Q6 for the traffic claim], no purchase of death-record data.
- **Price up front, refund without question, no auto-renew on the kit.** The vault and pre-need are opt-in annual plans with a cancel link in every email. (The FTC Funeral Rule's itemised-price norm is the industry's standard; its text was not fetched this pass [unknown: 16 CFR 453 not read].)
- **Data.** Templates of living people never leave the device unencrypted; no training on any face; deletion on request within 30 days (Everalbum order windows as the floor) [sourced: face-regulation.md Q5]; the deceased's archive is co-owned and exportable in full by every heir, so the company is never the only holder.
- **The honest counter-argument**: a product that reaches a bereaved person two weeks after a death and asks for $149 will be seen by some directors and families as predatory however it is framed. Falsifier: Load-bearing #6.

## Economics (per-customer COGS, GM, payback; cite research/unit-economics.md rows or derive with tags)

Reference estate = one 100 GB / 22,000-image decedent library [unit-economics.md Q2] plus three relatives' on-device indexes of their own libraries.

- **Recovery Kit ($149).** Ingest compute: embeddings + faces on the heir's Mac or a RunPod 4090 ($0.04–$0.11) [estimated: unit-economics.md Q3(a),(d)]; optional captions $0.69 batch [unit-economics.md Q3(b)]; dedupe CPU negligible. Support allowance $15 per estate [estimated: assumption — bereaved, non-technical users on a legal-ish process; this is the real COGS and a guess]. Contribution ≈ $149 − 4.62 − 0.85 − 2.09 − 0.10 − 15 ≈ **$126 (85%)** [estimated].
- **Concierge ($599).** Organizer labour 3 h × $75–$150 = $225–$450 [sourced rates: pro-channel.md Q2] paid out; plus the kit COGS → contribution **$126–$351 (21–59%)** [estimated]. Sold for the attach and the channel, not the margin.
- **Vault ($39.99/yr, index-only).** COGS ≈ $0.16/mo → **~86% GM at $4/mo** [estimated: unit-economics.md Q7 A-index-only row]; if the family keeps originals with us, single-copy B2 clears 59–74% at the 200 GB tier and replicated storage 23–53% [estimated: unit-economics.md Q7; research/assumptions/A5.md Against #3] — hence originals are optional and cold-tiered (hot previews + Deep Archive originals ≈ $0.0105/GB-mo → 66% GM at $4.99) [estimated: A5.md For #6].
- **B2B ($99/mo).** Hosting of a white-label page and dashboard ≈ $0; onboarding ~1 h; **~95% GM** [estimated].
- **Payback.** The kit is paid at purchase, so payback is immediate if CAC < $126; the organic channels (aftercare text, hospice, family) cost the home's goodwill, not cash. Paid B2B payback 7–9 months on the assumptions above. Vault payback at a $35 Qeepsake-like CAC is 13 months of contribution [estimated: 35 ÷ (39.99 × 0.79 ÷ 12); Qeepsake CAC $35 sourced: Tinybeans Q3 FY26 via customers.md Q1(h)].
- **Storage share of variable cost** collapses to transit + previews (<5% of kit revenue) because the archive's originals go back to the heirs' own iCloud/Google/drives by default; the "storage company" trap (unit-economics.md §3) is avoided by construction, at the cost of not being the system of record unless the family opts into the vault.
- **What breaks at 10k estates/yr.** Compute does not ($0.85 × 10k = $8.5k/yr). Support does: every path has a silent failure mode (sidecar mismatches, iCloud.com batch gaps, osxphotos "missing: 547", Apple export contents undocumented) and the customer is grieving; at $15/estate the support budget is $150k/yr for 10k estates, i.e. ~2 FTE — plausible only if the guided paths absorb most questions [estimated]. Legal exposure does if any living-person template ever reaches our servers unencrypted (Platform risk).

## Platform risk (the obvious feature Apple/Google/incumbent could ship; why this survives or doesn't)

- **Apple: "Legacy Contact → merge into your own iCloud Photos."** Today Apple's design is the opposite: a separate legacy account that is deleted after three years and no account merge exists on any Apple surface (A4 clause (a) holds, re-verified 2026-11-03; MacRumors iOS 27: "no changes to ... library merging, duplicates, or People/faces") [sourced: research/assumptions/A4.md For #1]. If Apple shipped "import your late parent's library with People" the Apple→Apple half of the kit evaporates; what survives is the cross-family dedupe, the siblings' libraries, Google-side and print-side material, the tribute pack, and the no-access majority. Probability by 2028 [unknown].
- **Google: name Photos explicitly in Inactive Account Manager and the deceased-user process, with a one-click transfer to the heir's account.** Today the IAM page does not name Photos and the deceased-user page publishes no format [sourced above]. Same survivor set as above.
- **Tukios is the real incumbent.** 10,000+ homes, 2.5M+ tribute videos, an "AI Photo Editor" and "Photo Engine", and an aftercare text product with AI reply monitoring [sourced: tukios.com, /aftercare, fetched 2026-11-03]. "Upload everything of Dad, we'll organize it" is one product cycle away for them, and they own the funeral-director relationship. Survival argument: Tukios sells to the home (per-video, per-site) and has no reason to build cross-account ingest, Takeout repair, Mac library readers or a consent architecture; the heir's archive is not the home's asset. Honest: if Tukios builds it, this variant becomes their feature or their acquisition, and the channel closes. Load-bearing #7.
- **Empathy / Everplans.** Empathy is logistics sold to insurers/employers, "Care Managers", LifeVault documents; no photo feature after $162M raised [sourced: empathy.com, fetched 2026-11-03]. Everplans' vault lists "Online Accounts" and "Digital Estate", no photos, base app free [sourced: https://www.everplans.com/, ©2026, fetched 2026-11-03]. Why this is not their feature: the work is photo engineering (Takeout sidecars, Photos.sqlite, HEIC/Live pairing, perceptual dedupe, face clustering under biometric law) with a support burden they have avoided for five years; the plausible outcome is partnership or acquisition, not competition. Why it is not a defence: a partnership they decline is a channel lost.
- **Ancestry/MyHeritage** already do deceased-only face matching inside a tree [sourced: competitive.md Q6]; they could add "import a deceased relative's iCloud export". Their customer is the genealogist, not the executor at week two.
- **Regulatory.** Naming the deceased is outside BIPA/CUBI on the reading in face-regulation.md (statutes protect living individuals; MyHeritage/Ancestry pattern) [sourced: face-regulation.md Q6–Q7, inference]; living relatives are named only after per-subject consent, templates for living people are computed on-device and synced only as ciphertext, and server matching is off in IL/TX/WA/EU [sourced: face-regulation.md Q7]. Residual: a court treating a deceased person's template as still-protected, or the Apple iCloud subclass prevailing on an encryption theory at trial (Law360 2026-10-09) [sourced: face-regulation.md Q1] — [unknown].
- **Net:** survives a single platform move and the Empathy/Everplans non-move; does not survive Tukios shipping it with Apple simultaneously closing the Apple→Apple gap. Rubric honesty: 5/10.

## Compounding asset

- **The family archive outlives the estate.** Unlike every one-time product in the comparables table (Legacybox gates viewing behind $79.99/yr after 30 days; iMemories after 30 days) [sourced: pro-channel.md Q5], the archive is the heirs' and persists after the kit is paid; the *second* death in the same family (the surviving parent; an older sibling) starts from a named, deduplicated base, and the deceased's identity graph and provenance ledger are already there. Nobody else holds "which of the seven copies is the original and who confirmed it was Grandpa".
- **The pre-need network.** Each paid estate puts 2–4 living relatives [assumption] through the exact failure they want to avoid; they set Legacy Contact / IAM with our walkthrough and designate the archive's next owner — which makes their own future export land in the same archive. It is slow (deaths per family per decade are few) and it is the only compounding mechanism with a built-in second event.
- **Consent and provenance records** for living relatives (BIPA 15(b) written releases, deceased flags) are both a legal asset and a switching cost [sourced: face-regulation.md Q7].
- **Channel trust**: a funeral home that has sent 200 families to a product that behaved well is slow to switch; the dashboard history is theirs.
- **Honest limits.** The index is rebuildable for cents; MCP is a commodity (54,763 active registry servers) [sourced: byo-ai.md Q5]; the deceased's photos go back into the heirs' own Apple/Google libraries by design, so switching cost on the archive itself is deliberately low unless they take the vault. What compounds is the human-confirmed layer and the channel, and both grow at the pace of deaths in enrolled families.

## Buildability (4-week demo spec for 2 engineers; ugliest technical risk)

**Demo (macOS 27 + iOS 27 + web; two engineers).**
- Week 1 — Paths and ingest: the four-path triage flow (static, sourced to Apple/Google articles); web upload of zips/folders; Mac ingest of a privacy.apple.com export, iCloud.com batch folders, Takeout with sidecar repair (immich-go's matching rules reimplemented; AGPL code not linked) [sourced: github.com/simulot/immich-go via variants/v1-organizer.md], and a Photos.sqlite reader (osxphotos as reference; licence to verify [unknown]); provenance ledger; exact-hash dedupe.
- Week 2 — Identity and dedupe: AdaFace (MIT) via Core ML + clustering; "seed the deceased with three photos"; perceptual and edit/original pairing; tribute-pack export (JPEG, chronological, 30–100 picks with a simple quality/diversity ranker).
- Week 3 — Relatives: iOS app that indexes the heir's own library on-device (MobileCLIP-S2 + Vision faces; `BGContinuedProcessingTask` with Live Activity) [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q2–Q3] and contributes only the deceased's cluster (previews + vectors) to the estate space; invite link; cross-library dedupe; naming UI with deceased flag and per-living-person consent screen.
- Week 4 — Outputs and channel: co-owned archive with full export per heir (folder + XMP PersonInImage/MWG; Apple Photos import with albums + name keywords); white-label funeral-home page and dashboard; one real run on a volunteer family with a real privacy.apple.com export.
- Acceptance: a real Apple Data & Privacy export plus two relatives' phones produce one archive with ≥99.5% of input items traced in the ledger [estimated threshold: osxphotos' "missing: 547" of 49,231 = 1.1% is the silent-loss rate to beat] and a tribute pack in ≤1 h of heir touch-time; the Apple export's contents (albums? People names? edit/original pairs?) are documented from the run — today they are [unknown].

**Ugliest technical risk: we do not know what the platform hands over, and the customer cannot retry.** Apple's export page says only "original formats" and "14 days" [sourced: support.apple.com/102208]; whether albums, People names, edited versions and Live Photo pairs are in the privacy.apple.com bundle, how big it is, and how long it takes to prepare are all [unknown]; the Legacy Contact flow can only be exercised with a real death or a cooperating living account, and the court-order path cannot be tested at all before launch. Google's deceased-user delivery format is [unknown]. The 14-day window plus a grieving, non-technical heir plus a 100 GB download is the failure mode. Second: Photos.sqlite changes every September (four breakages in 2026) [sourced: A2.md Against #6]. Third: the no-access majority means the first version must deliver value from the living relatives' phones alone, which is the v1-federation build with all its risks (ANE background entitlement undocumented; share of assets with no local copy [unknown]) [sourced: ios-feasibility.md Q2, Q4]. Kill criterion: in a 20-family pilot recruited through two funeral homes, if fewer than 50% of families can produce *any* bulk export of the decedent's library within 60 days, the kit is a checklist, and the product collapses to the free tribute pack plus the federation plan.

## Load-bearing assumptions (numbered; each with the observable fact that would falsify it)

1. **≥20% of smartphone decedents leave an heir with some export path** (Legacy Contact, passcode, Mac). Falsified if intake data on the first 500 estates shows <15% with any path; or a nat-rep survey puts "know a parent's phone passcode" below 15%.
2. **Heirs will pay $149 at 2–12 weeks after the death.** Falsified if <2% of aftercare-text recipients who opened the free pack buy within 90 days, or <30 of ~100 interviewed executors pay a $99 deposit (A3.md flip condition).
3. **Funeral homes will carry a third-party photo offer in their aftercare.** Falsified if <5 of the first 50 homes pitched enroll, or if enrolled homes' directors remove the link within 6 months.
4. **Apple's privacy.apple.com export delivers the library in a usable form (originals, dates, ideally albums).** Falsified by the week-4 run showing missing albums/edits or a bundle that cannot be downloaded in the 14-day window by a non-technical user.
5. **Google's deceased-user "obtain data" outcome includes Photos in a Takeout-like archive within ~90 days.** [unknown today]; falsified by three documented requests returning no Photos or taking >6 months.
6. **The offer is accepted as a service, not predation.** Falsified if >5% of kit buyers request refunds citing timing, or if funeral directors in the pilot name it as a reason to drop the link, or if App Store / Trustpilot reviews are predominantly negative on timing.
7. **Tukios (or Tribute Technology) does not ship "recover and organise the deceased's photos" within 12 months.** Falsified by a product page or release note on tukios.com describing bulk photo collection/organisation for families beyond the tribute video.
8. **Naming the deceased carries no biometric-consent duty; living relatives consent per subject; on-device templates keep the company outside BIPA/CUBI/GDPR Art. 9 possession.** Falsified by a ruling applying BIPA/CUBI to a deceased subject's template, an Illinois state court rejecting G.T. v. Samsung, or an EU DPA decision against a consumer app's face clustering [face-regulation.md Q1, Q4].
9. **Enough photos of the deceased exist on the living relatives' phones for the free tribute pack to be useful without the decedent's library.** Falsified if <50% of pilot families produce ≥30 usable photos from relatives' phones alone.
10. **Vault attach ≥20% and retention nearer the Backblaze regime than the Photo & Video regime.** Falsified if attach <10% in the first 500 kits, or first-year vault renewal <40%.
11. **Pre-need conversion ≥5% of exposed relatives.** Falsified if <2% after 12 months (prior: only 12% of US adults have any account-level control, Boomers 4% [sourced: T&W 2026]).
12. **The Photos.sqlite / Takeout ingest can be kept lossless across OS releases with two engineers.** Falsified if any September release leaves the Mac path broken >14 days or a pilot family reports an unexplained missing item.
13. **The ceiling is accepted as ~$16–55M US unless the vault becomes the family plan.** Falsified upward only by EU/UK legal paths proving as clean as Apple's US Legacy Contact flow, or by a Tukios/Empathy-scale distribution deal.
