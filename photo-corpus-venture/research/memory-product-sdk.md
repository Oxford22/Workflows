# Gap track R10 — B2B demand for an ingestion + AI-curation SDK from memory-product companies

Written 2026-11-03; repaired 2026-11-03 after hostile audit (see "Audit log"). Tags per README: `[sourced: url, date]`, `[sourced-secondary: ...]`, `[estimated: method]`, `[unknown]`. All URLs accessed 2026-11-03 unless noted. Prior context: Google removed self-serve library read on 2025-03-31 (research/platform-access.md Q4; research/assumptions/A1.md).

**Bottom line (stated up front):** The buyers exist and are large, but (a) every named buyer that cares has already *built* curation on free platform primitives (Apple Vision aesthetics on iOS 18+, CEWE's in-house AI campus, Shutterfly Autofill "Best Photos", Chatbooks "Smart Select", Mixbook Studio/Story Mode, Google/Fujifilm using Google Photos' own intelligence); (b) the Google removal was absorbed by swapping to the Picker with user-visible friction, and no buyer has published a funnel hit; (c) the SDK shape that makes the product interesting — **one on-device index shared across different companies' host apps**, with consented family contribution — is not implementable on iOS (App Group containers are per-developer-team) and is implementable on Android only as an exported ContentProvider that each host must still justify under Play's photo-permission policy. A *per-host* SDK (each host app re-indexes its own grant) is technically permitted on both platforms; whether that reduced shape is worth buying is a product judgement, and the Q5 evidence says the buyers build it themselves. The round-2 SDK variant should be killed or re-shaped as a first-party app that *exports* to these companies, not a library they embed.

## 1. Questions asked

1. Who are the buyers and how big (revenue, photo-selection step, mobile share)?
2. How did they respond to the Google Library API removal; evidence it hurt funnels?
3. Is photo selection the funnel killer; what curation exists; third-party "best photo" APIs and Apple's PhotoKit aesthetic score?
4. SDK precedents and pricing per 1k images.
5. Would they buy vs build (engineering scale, AI launches 2024–2026)?
6. Platform-policy risk for a full-Photos-access SDK on iOS and Android; cross-app index feasibility.
7. Ceiling arithmetic inputs (full-access app counts, industry revenue, the "$3.8B" claim, plausible per-order/per-MAU fee vs margins).

### Q2. Response to the Google Library API removal

Falsification target: "they lost the Google route." Partly false — they kept it via the Picker, with new friction:

- Chatbooks published "Why has adding Google Photos Changed?": "Google Photos limits each session to one hour"; "Google photos will not keep track of previously selected photos."; Google may prompt to pick up to 2,000 but only 60 print; advice is to pre-build an album in Google Photos [sourced: https://help.chatbooks.com/en/articles/10760721-why-has-adding-google-photos-changed, "updated over 3 weeks ago"]. A second article: "There's no warning inside Google Photos when time is running low," so uploads fail silently; workaround "Create an album in Google Photos first" [sourced: https://help.chatbooks.com/en/articles/16110757-why-does-my-google-photos-time-out-after-i-m-done-selecting-photos]. The 2,000 cap and 60-minute URL lifetime match Picker API limits in platform-access.md Q4, so Chatbooks is on the Picker.
- Shutterfly: help article still describes Google import ("Select photos and Click 'Add'"); "Shutterfly is unable to control which photos or albums are displayed once connected"; fallback: download to a computer and upload [sourced: https://support.shutterfly.com/s/article/Import-from-Google-Photos, page date 2026-05-14]. Blog: "You can select up to 1,000 photos in one session" [sourced: shutterfly.com/ideas/how-to-make-a-photo-book-from-google-photos, 2026-02-24]. A 2024 blog post reported Shutterfly "unable to import from Google Photos until an agreement is reached" [sourced-secondary: geeksontour.com forum via search snippet, ~762 days old; fetch returned empty] — a documented disruption window, pre-dating the March 2025 cutoff.
- Snapfish: help article "Unable to upload via Google Photos" exists (revoke and re-grant access) [sourced-secondary: support.snapfish.com via search; fetch returned 403].
- Mixbook: help center blocked (403); marketing pages still list Google Photos as an import source [sourced-secondary: mixbook.com via search]. The 2026-11 homepage and Studio page do not mention Google Photos at all [sourced: mixbook.com, mixbook.com/studio, 2026-11-03].
- **App release notes (new in this pass).** Current "What's New" via the iTunes Search API, 2026-11-03: Shutterfly v16.20.1 (2026-10-28) "Bug fixes and performance improvements"; Chatbooks v5.17.9 (2026-10-29) "Performance improvements, app enhancements."; Mixbook v5.7.0 (2026-11-02) "Meet the new Mixbook app. Experience your memories in more ways than ever. Rediscover your best moments..."; Snapfish v17.17.0 (2026-10-30) new gifts, spine titles, global search [sourced: https://itunes.apple.com/search?term=...&entity=software]. None mentions Google Photos. Full version histories were not retrievable (the App Store page loads history client-side; the API returns only the current notes) [unknown for 2025 releases]. The FreePrints (PlanetArt) description still advertises "select the photos you want to print from your Camera Roll, Google Photos, Facebook, Dropbox and more" [sourced: same API] — so at least one large printer still routes through the Picker.
- Evidence it hurt funnels: **none published** [unknown]. Shutterfly's −8% Q1 2026 revenue and widening EBITDA loss [sourced: Yahoo, 2026-06-10] are not attributed to Google by any source; the bond documents are reported as acknowledging that "increased video consumption and AI-enabled image creation will impact the business" — a risk-factor disclosure, not an explanation of the −8% [sourced: same]. Support-forum / app-review complaints: Reddit searches returned nothing in the first pass (site: filter ignored twice); this pass could not search at all (budget) and App Store review text is not exposed by the Search API [unknown].

Read: the Google route degraded from "connect library" to "pick ≤2,000 in a 60-minute window, no memory." Vendors absorbed it with help articles, not product changes. That is weak evidence of pain and no evidence of willingness to pay to fix it.

### Q4. SDK precedents and pricing

- Media APIs: Cloudinary states "more than 4M developers and 13,000 brands" and "founded in 2012"; no revenue on its site [sourced: https://cloudinary.com/about]; revenue is estimates only ($77M–$220M; $100M ARR in 2021 per BVP) [sourced-secondary: getlatka/rocketreach/zoominfo via search] [unknown exact]. imgix: "starter" "$25/mo" "Up to 50GB of media"/"Up to 100GB delivery bandwidth"; "basic" $75/mo; "MIDRANGE" $150/mo; "growth" $300/mo; "growth plus" $500/mo; "enterprise" Custom [sourced: https://imgix.com/pricing] — priced on storage/bandwidth, not per image.
- Photographer culling AI (closest analogue to "best photos of X"): Aftershoot culling-only from ~$10/mo annual, flat fee, unlimited images [sourced-secondary: aftershoot.com/blog/aftershoot-pricing-tiers via search]. Narrative Select: Lite $15/mo ($10 annual), Standard $29/$20, Premium $59/$40, Ultra $79/$60; "Every plan includes unlimited projects and unlimited images" [sourced: https://narrative.so/pricing]. Imagen: editing "Starting at US$0.05 / photo", pay-as-you-go culling "From US$12/mo", Limitless $129/mo annual [sourced: https://imagen-ai.com/pricing]. Pattern: culling is sold flat-rate, not per image, because on-device inference has ~zero marginal cost.
- Picker APIs: Google Picker quota "100,000 requests per minute per project"; Library API "10,000 requests per project per day" [sourced: https://developers.google.com/photos/overview/api-limits-quotas]. No pricing statement on the Picker get-started page [sourced: https://developers.google.com/photos/picker/guides/get-started-picker] — treated as free-at-quota [estimated: absence of any billing text on Google's Photos API pages]. Apple's picker (`PHPickerViewController`) is free and out-of-process (platform-access.md Q1). Dropbox Chooser: "a small JavaScript component that enables your app to get files from Dropbox"; returns file objects with links; direct links "will expire after four hours"; no pricing on the page [sourced: https://docs.dropboxapi.com/dropbox-api/docs/pre-built-components/chooser]. Box Content Picker "allows developers to add support for selecting files and folders from Box"; no pricing on the page [sourced: https://developer.box.com/guides/embed/ui-elements/picker/]. All four pickers are free, per-item, user-mediated — the market price of "let a user pick photos from a cloud" is zero.

### Q6. Platform-policy risk for the SDK shape — this is the killer for the *shared-index* shape

- **Cross-app index on iOS is not possible across companies.** "An app group allows multiple apps developed by the same team to access one or more shared containers" [sourced: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-app-groups.md]. OWASP MASTG (now fetched in full): "Different developer teams cannot use the same app group." and "App Groups do not enable sharing between apps from different developer teams." [sourced: https://mas.owasp.org/MASTG/knowledge/ios/MASVS-PLATFORM/MASTG-KNOW-0125/, fetched via curl 2026-11-03]. Consequence: an SDK embedded in Shutterfly and in Chatbooks builds two separate indexes, each needing its own full-library grant; nothing is "indexed once." The only cross-app sharing primitives are user-mediated and per-item (share sheet, document picker, `Transferable` drag/drop, App Intents entities exposed by the owning app) [sourced: Apple App Intents and Transferable docs, Q4]. **A per-host index is permitted**: nothing stops each host app from running the SDK against its own grant; what is lost is the "index once, reuse everywhere" economics and the family-contribution graph spanning apps.
- **Cross-app index on Android is technically possible, but the host still owns the grant.** App data "isn't normally accessible to other packages" [sourced: https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/permissions/defining]. A ContentProvider can expose it: "By default, all applications can read from or write to your provider, even if the underlying data is private, because by default your provider doesn't have permissions set"; access is restricted with custom `<permission>` elements and `android:exported` [sourced: https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/providers/content-provider-creating]. `signature` permissions are granted "only if the requesting application is signed with the same certificate as the application that declared the permission"; since Android 12 `knownSigner` grants "only if the requesting application is signed with an allowed certificate" listed at declaration time [sourced: https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/manifest/permission-element; .../permissions/defining]. So an SDK vendor could ship one "index provider" app and allow-list each partner's signing certificate — a cross-publisher index is feasible on Android. But: the index content comes from `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES`, whose Play policy allows the permission only when pickers "are not sufficient for your app to provide core functionality"; "Apps whose core functionality revolves around broad access to Photos and Videos may use the permissions"; mandatory since 2025-05-28 [sourced: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180]. A standalone "index provider" app with no user-facing photo product has a weak core-functionality claim [estimated: reading of the policy text; Google has not ruled on this shape]. Android is also the smaller half of this market: Mixbook has no Android app at all [sourced: mixbook.com], and the iOS rating counts dwarf Play review counts for every buyer in the table.
- **The host, not the SDK, requests full Photos access.** On iOS the permission prompt and purpose string belong to the host app; the SDK's library index needs `.authorized` (platform-access.md Q1). Apple 5.1.1(iii): "Apps should only request access to data relevant to the core functionality of the app... Where possible, use the out-of-process picker or a share sheet rather than requesting full access to protected resources like Photos or Contacts" [sourced: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/]. 5.1.1(i): privacy policy must "Confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data... such as analytics tools, advertising networks and third-party SDKs... will provide the same or equal protection"; 5.1.2(i): "You must clearly disclose where personal data will be shared with third parties, including with third-party AI, and obtain explicit permission before doing so" [sourced: same]. Apple's own developer guidance (WWDC20 session 10641, transcript now fetched): "ask yourself if your app even needs to ask the user for access to their photo library at all. For most apps the answer is no." and "Of course there are apps that provide a very photo-centric experience that require full access. For example, browsing apps, editing apps, camera apps, or data backup apps." [sourced: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10641/, 2020]. A photo-book app can argue it is photo-centric; the argument is the host's to make, in its own purpose string.
- **SDK compliance overhead.** A third-party SDK must ship `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` if it "collects data about the person using apps that include the third-party SDK, enables the app to collect data about people using the app, or contacts tracking domains," declaring collected data types and required-reason APIs [sourced: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files.md]. Listed SDKs also need signatures; "you are responsible for all the code the SDK includes in your app" [sourced: https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/]. Family-contribution networking from inside a host app makes the SDK a data processor the host must disclose under 5.1.2(i).
- **Android host liability for SDKs.** Library manifests merge into the app's: "the Gradle build merges all manifest files into a single manifest file that's packaged into your app"; `<uses-permission>` elements merge by `android:name` [sourced: https://developer.android.com/build/manage-manifests]. So an SDK declaring `READ_MEDIA_IMAGES` makes the *host* the declarant. Play User Data policy: "If you include third party code (for example, an SDK) in your app, you must ensure that the third party code used in your app, and that third party's practices with respect to user data from your app, are compliant with Google Play Developer Program policies, which include use and disclosure requirements."; "This requirement applies regardless of whether user data is transferred after being sent to a server, or by embedding third-party code in your app." [sourced: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311]. The photo-permission policy itself does not mention SDKs — the host owns the justification [sourced: answer/14115180].
- **Verdict for Q6, stated plainly.** Platform fact: on iOS there is no sanctioned way for an SDK to hold one library index that different companies' apps share; on Android there is (exported ContentProvider + knownSigner/custom permission), subject to a Play core-functionality justification that a headless index app would struggle to make. Platform fact: a per-host SDK (each host indexes under its own full-access grant) is allowed on both platforms, at the cost of each host taking on full-access justification, privacy-manifest disclosure, and third-party-AI disclosure. Product judgement (ours, not Apple's or Google's): the per-host shape strips out the "index once, consented family contribution across apps" differentiation that justified the variant, leaving an on-device curation library that Q3/Q5 show the buyers already build on free primitives. The surviving shape is a standalone first-party app (one index, one grant) that exports selections *to* these vendors via share sheet / their upload APIs — i.e., the reverse of an SDK.
