## Stance

I am the payer this variant describes: 38, children aged 3 and 6, parents aged 72 and 75 in another state and not on social media. I send photos by text. I have made two photo books in five years while meaning to make one a year. I pay for iCloud+. My sibling never sends our parents anything. I score on what I would do, what I would give up, who I would trust, what happens if the company dies, what it costs against what I do now, and whether I would tell a friend. Pain, Wedge and Distribution are scored hardest.

## What this looks like from my kitchen table

**Would I do the first step?** Probably, once, at a gift moment, at $4.95. Ten minutes and a proof of issue 1 is less work than the books I do not make. The hesitation is the photo-permission prompt: full access to every photo of my children, for a company I have never heard of, to print 12. "Only approved images leave the phone" helps; I would still tap "limited" if offered, and the variant says that under any fallback "the product is Famileo with nicer captions".

**What do I give up?** $143.88 a year [estimated: 11.99 × 12], every year. An Aura Carver is $149 once, $139 this week (family-artifact-market.md Q1); the journal passes it in month 13 [estimated: 149 ÷ 11.99 = 12.4] and costs $719 over five years [estimated: 143.88 × 5]. I do not give up iCloud+, which is right: this does not ask to be my system of record.

**Would my parents take part?** Opening an envelope: yes, and gladly; that half of the wedge is real. Calling a printed number to tell a recording about a photo: my mother perhaps once, my father no. They would call me. Nothing in the research says otherwise: no usage figure exists for any phone interviewer with people over 70, and the nearest proxy is about 10 stories per family against 52 prompts (memoir-gift-market.md Q2, Q5). The variant's own threshold (30% call at least once) is a hope.

**Would my sibling take part?** No. The variant concedes it: "a relative who ignores the push contributes nothing." So the journal is my photos, paid for by me, which is what my texts already are.

**Who do I trust, and what if the company dies?** The issues on my parents' shelf survive any shutdown; that is the best thing about paper. The recordings of my parents' voices do not survive unless export works on the day it matters. The variant says export "must always work" and offers no mechanism (escrow, automatic copies to my own storage, a named successor). The memoir category's failure list is long (memoir-gift-market.md Q8: eight of seventeen closed, insolvent, pivoted or doubtful), and one of them now releases recordings "by email request only".

**Would I recommend it?** To a friend whose parent has no smartphone, no Wi-Fi or lives in care: yes, if issue 1 landed well. To a friend whose parents text: I would say buy the frame.

## Most damaging objection

**For the household this variant names as its customer, the paid job is already done, and the only parts that are new depend on the two people who do not act.**

My parents are 72 and 75; like 79% of people over 65 they have smartphones, and they are in the 46% who text with family (family-artifact-market.md Q5). Every photo I would approve for the journal is one I have already texted. So the journal's claim on $144 a year is three things: paper, my sibling's photos, and my parents' replies. Paper is real and small. My sibling's photos need my sibling to tap, which the variant admits will not happen for a relative who ignores the push. My parents' replies need them to phone a recording, for which no evidence exists at any age over 70. Remove those two and I am paying a subscription for a slower copy of my own text thread, against a $149 frame with no fee that four relatives can post to (Aura: "4 average contributors per frame", "No subscription fees").

The US record says other parents reached the same conclusion. Famileo has sold this at $9.99–13.99 in the US since at least 2021 and has 110 US iOS ratings against 5,322 in France; Neveo has 94; the two US start-ups claim 520 and 1,000 families, one after a national TV-deals placement; Recently, which already prints a monthly book from a shared iCloud album, has 1,224 ratings after eleven years (family-artifact-market.md Q2, Q5). The variant's answer is that proposal removes the selection effort, but neither scaled operator names selection as its constraint (Q4 verdict), and proposal does nothing for the sibling or the reply.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2).** The variant states it: $4–16M, "$100M does not pencil". The repaired research is harsher on the inputs beneath that range (margin [unknown], comparable growth +2% to +9%, care-home channel flat). From the customer's side the cause is plain: the people for whom paper beats a screen (no smartphone, no Wi-Fi, in care) are a residue whose size the variant marks [unknown], and for everyone else a one-time frame is enough.

## Strongest dimension

**Buildability (6).** Scoping the phone's work to one month of photos removes the two problems that sank earlier variants' builds: no library migration and no long background job (ios-feasibility.md Q2–Q3). Twenty families and two issues can be produced by two engineers with a local printer in four weeks, and that pilot would answer the questions that matter to me (did my parents call; did my sibling tap; did I approve issue 2 in two minutes). The strongest single product fact, though, is on the elder's side of the Wedge: the recipient has to do nothing but open the mail.

## Three questions for the founder

1. If my parents record their voices with you and you close, or I stop paying, what exactly do I receive, in what format, how soon, and who is obliged to deliver it? Will you put that in writing and copy every recording to my own storage as it is made?
2. Have you run print-and-mail operations before (address changes, reprints, December peaks, a postal permit), and how many months of runway do you have to reach a contract print quote and a USPS ruling before the 59% margin is a fact and not a cell in a table?
3. My parents text and have Wi-Fi, and my sibling will not tap anything. What do I get in month 13 that a $149 frame and my text thread do not give me, and what would you change if the pilot shows fewer than one recipient in three ever calls the number?
