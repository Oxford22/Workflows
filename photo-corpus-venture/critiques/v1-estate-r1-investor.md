# v1-estate — Round 1 critique — Investor lens

Date: 2026-11-03. Critic: fresh-context, cold seed investor. Read: SCORECARD.md, variants/v1-estate.md, every file in research/ and research/assumptions/. Did not read CHANGELOG, other variants, or other critiques.

## Stance

I see 500 decks a year and my default is no. I want to know who pays, how much, why now, what the wedge is, what the distribution is, what must be true for a $1B outcome, and whether the founder is fooling themselves about platform risk. I am allergic to storage-company economics (this variant avoids that, credit where due), to consumer products that need behavior change, and to small channels dressed up as markets. I scored Ceiling, Distribution and Platform risk hardest, as instructed.

Summary verdict: this is the most honest variant I have read in the search — the author writes "this variant tops out at a few tens of millions in the US" and "it is a trigger, not a market" in their own ceiling section. I agree with them. That honesty is why the conversation would be short. The product is a well-specified $16–55M business (on the author's own assumption bands) selling into a channel whose incumbent (Tukios) already owns the exact photo moment the free wedge targets, with a paid product addressed to a slice of decedents that nobody can size (Apple Legacy Contact adoption is [unknown]; the only proxy is 4% of Boomers).

## Most damaging objection

**Tukios already gives each of its 10,000+ funeral homes a free text/email upload link that collects photos and videos from relatives' phones for the tribute video — which is your free wedge — and already sends the two-week and six-week aftercare texts you want to ride. Name one funeral home that has agreed to put a third-party photo link in its aftercare text, and tell me what its director said when you explained that the family already uploaded their photos to Tukios the week before.**

Why this ends the conversation: the variant's only non-paid channel is the funeral-home aftercare touchpoint; its only free first-use case is "photos of Dad from everyone's phones, deduplicated, in the format the funeral home wants" — and estate.md Q6/Q8 shows the home's existing vendor collects exactly those photos, from exactly those phones, by exactly that mechanism, with no app install, and the research's own observation is that "A new entrant would be a feature inside Tukios/Tribute, not a channel partner." If the founder cannot name a home, there is no wedge and no distribution, and the Recovery Kit is a direct-to-consumer product for an unsizable minority with [unknown] search volume.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2).** The author's own ceiling is $16–55M US ARR at maturity on unsourced conversion assumptions, and the three routes to $100M are (i) a 27–31% penetration of all smartphone decedents that no bereavement product has approached (Empathy: 2M families in ~5 years through 8 of 10 top insurers), (ii) turning the vault into the living family's plan, which is the v1-federation ceiling problem wearing a different coat, or (iii) EU/UK, where the legal paths are [unknown]. sizing.md's verdict — "a trigger, not a market" — stands. A seed investor does not fund a trigger.
