## Stance

I run data partnerships at a company that trains image and video models. I have signed one stock-agency licence and one broker deal. I am measured on rights cleanliness, provenance, diversity, exclusivity and price per usable item. Anything containing people needs legal sign-off. I was burned once by a set that turned out to hold scraped and AI-generated images. I also judge whether the people who are not the payer (phone owners supplying photos) would take part.

Short answer from that seat: I would take the call and run the free count query. I would not issue the purchase order. The supplier side would install for cash and mostly not for an archive.

### Notes on the three dimensions scored hardest

**Pain (3).** My real pain is provenance and people. After a contaminated set I will pay more for evidence that a file came off a real camera, and I pay most for content with people that carries releases, because that is where my legal sign-off is hard. This variant sells neither: it excludes all people, and for the iPhone (its only v1 platform) it offers no capture signature, only a neighbour-based score the variant itself calls forgeable and unmeasured. People-free scenery is what I already hold from stock at $0.02–0.04 with human captions (Q1 Freepik; Q3 Shutterstock CEO on "legally obtained images with good descriptions"). Research finds the pattern from the sell side: Kled's upload button "could only collect the data people already had, not the data labs actually needed" (Q3).

**Wedge (3).** Would I do the first step? Yes: a count query and a 1,000-item sample cost me nothing. That is real credit. Would I do the second? Each of these stops me:
- The licence is revocable through a feed I must honour. My current vendors do the opposite: Kled's licence is "worldwide, perpetual and irrevocable" and exclusive for AI uses; Troveo signs 95% of licensors exclusively (Q2).
- No exclusivity, so no advantage over a peer who buys the same set.
- No indemnity I can rely on from a company funded with about $2.4M. Getty indemnifies its customers for release failures (Q4).
- My counsel will not accept "people-free with a measured miss rate" without running our own screen. At a 0.1% miss rate a 10M-item set holds 10,000 unreleased people [estimated: 10M × 0.001].
- If the startup dies, the consent ledger and the revocation feed die with it, and my EU training-content summary points at a vendor that no longer exists. The variant specifies no escrow.

Would the suppliers take part? Installs, yes, when cash leads: Neon took 75,000 downloads in a day (Q2). Careful participation, no. Only about 3.9% of CNIL's sample would sell data for under EUR 1 a month and the modal ask is EUR 10–30 a month (Q5); the variant offers $6 once, and the repaired research $9–25 once. Kled's one-star reviews complain about exactly what this app asks for: full photo-library access and pay that does not arrive (Q2). Reviewing 628 to 1,414 items one screen at a time, after an ID check, is a large ask for that money. The variant's own kill threshold is a 10% opt-in; I would expect it to be missed among people who came for a private archive.

**Distribution (2).** Two channels are needed and neither is evidenced. To reach me: direct sales to six to a few dozen accounts, with a legal and security review whose length the research does not measure [unknown]. To reach suppliers: the cash hook brings referral promoters and fraud; the archive hook has no evidenced channel (Ente has 490,639 registered accounts in total, per SCORECARD); the organizer channel from current.md does not carry over, as the variant says. On repaired inputs paid acquisition could pay back in one sale (about $30 contribution against a $14 CAC [estimated: 70.70 − 35.35 − about 5 of costs]), but only at a $0.05 price that no buyer has paid for this asset.

## Most damaging objection

The offer removes what I pay a premium for and adds an obligation I do not accept. It is a non-exclusive, revocable, people-free, uncaptioned, iPhone-only stills set, with provenance that is a forgeable score, from a vendor that cannot indemnify me. A competitor already lists a 200M-file consented camera-roll corpus today under a perpetual, irrevocable, exclusive licence, and on 2026-09-30 that competitor stopped leading with it because labs wanted capture to specification instead (ai-data-licensing.md Q2, Q3). The variant's single commercial test is "a written price from any buyer for a people-free camera-roll set". From this seat I would not write one. I would run the free count query, ask for people with releases, video, and capture to my specification, and the company that can answer that is a task marketplace that needs no archive.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2), tied with Distribution (2).** Ceiling is the one the research settles: the evidenced range is $4–24M gross, it is transaction revenue, and the company keeps about half. Distribution is the one that would fail first in practice.

## Strongest dimension

**Buildability (5).** The index-in-place engine uses documented APIs (PhotoKit previews, Foundation Models tools, App Attest), storage of opted-in originals costs cents per supplier, and nothing needs a platform partnership. It is the strongest only because the hard parts are commercial. One design idea is worth keeping from a buyer's view: matching against a listed index before any upload, with library-context evidence in the manifest. If its catch rate were measured it would address the exact failure that burned me.

## Unknowns research could resolve before the next round

1. Do any training-content summaries published under EU AI Act Art. 53(1)(d) since 2026-08-02, or any lab dataset cards, name a consumer camera-roll or user-contributed personal-photo source (Kled, Wirestock, GoPro)? This is the missing buyer-side evidence.
2. What did GoPro's AI licensing program earn after Q2 2026, at what price per hour, with how many hours actually licensed and what opt-in rate among invited subscribers (Q4 2026 release and FY2026 10-K)?
3. Do any 2025–2026 training-data licences (Shutterstock, Getty, Troveo, Kled, Wirestock, GoPro terms) include a contributor revocation or deletion feed that the buyer must honour, or are all perpetual and irrevocable?
4. What recall do current person, face and minor detectors reach on small and background people in consumer photos (published benchmarks), and do lab procurement terms require a zero-miss warranty for "people-free" sets?
5. Is there priced demand for provenance-verified real-camera photo sets as the negative class for synthetic-image detection (price and buyers of Kled's "Camera-Original Phone Photos" or similar sets)?

## Three questions for the founder

1. Can you name one data-partnerships lead who will put a price in writing for a 1M-item, people-free, non-exclusive, revocable camera-roll stills set, and what did that person say about the revocation feed and indemnity?
2. Are you prepared to register as a California data broker, carry buyer indemnities, and have "sells family photos" attached to a product that every earlier variant positioned as private? Do you have the counsel and insurance budget for it?
3. When buyers answer "we want people with releases, video, and capture to our specification", will you build the task marketplace (Kled V3, Wirestock), or is that a company you do not want to run? How many months of runway cover a lab's legal and security review before the first purchase order?
