# Research track R14: institutional payers for life-story / family-photo programmes

Access date for every URL below: 2026-11-24. Tags follow README.md. "Search extract" means the figure came from a search-result extract of the named page and the page itself was not fetched or was blocked; those are tagged sourced-secondary.

## Bottom line

1. The evidence does not support a nine-figure outcome for an institutional life-story product sold per resident or per community. No vendor in the category has a sourced revenue of $10M; the largest sourced figure is LifeBio at "over $2 million in revenue with 48 employees" about two decades after founding [sourced-secondary: https://practicalfounders.com/podcast/43-helping-seniors-tell-their-stories-and-fight-loneliness-with-easy-apps-beth-sanders/, 2023-05-05].
2. Every other dedicated vendor shrank, pivoted or stayed charity-sized: Storii became an EHR for adult day care (StoriiCare), MemoryWell left senior living and its site now resolves to a redirect stub, My Life Films reports £235,652 income [sourced: https://register-of-charities.charitycommission.gov.uk/en/charity-search/-/charity-details/5045434, FY to 2025-02-28].
3. The buyer exists and is counted (41,465 assisted-living communities, 14,742 nursing facilities, 6,535 hospices), and federal rules do name "life history", but the mandate is met with an intake form, and the dominant engagement vendor (LifeLoop, 4,700+ communities) already ships a "Life Story" feature inside its platform.
4. Price evidence clusters at $2,000-$10,000 per community per year for life story and $39-$650 per community per month for engagement tools; at LifeLoop-level penetration (about 8% of facilities) that yields roughly $8M-$28M [estimated: section 2, Q8], inside the $5-45M cap already found for consumer variants.
5. The only adjacent business above $10M is Famileo (near €14M in 2024), a family-news gazette where the institution pays a small fee and the family photo flow is the product; it is evidence for family-photo engagement in care homes, not for life-story capture, and it took about ten years to reach that figure in its home market.

## 1. Questions asked

Q1 market structure and counts. Q2 what operators already buy for engagement and what they pay. Q3 life-story vendors in institutions and their scale. Q4 regulatory and quality drivers and the evidence base. Q5 sales reality. Q6 family as payer inside the institution. Q7 insurer / employer / pre-need channel. Q8 falsification: do these programmes stay tiny.

## 2. Findings

### Q1. Market structure

- Assisted living (US): 41,465 communities, 1,369,317 licensed units, 1,016,424 residents, 33 units on average; size mix 43% small (4-10 units), 14% medium (11-25), 33% large (26-100), 10% over 100; 44% of residents have Alzheimer's or other dementia; average yearly cost $74,400; 512,300 employees [sourced: https://www.ahcancal.org/Advocacy/State%20Data/US_AL_Fast_Facts_2026.pdf, June 2026]. 57.3% chain-affiliated, 42.7% independent [sourced: https://www.ahcancal.org/Assisted-Living/Facts-and-Figures/Pages/default.aspx, undated page, read 2026-11-24].

### Q4. Regulatory and quality drivers

- Nursing homes: the comprehensive assessment must cover "a resident's needs, strengths, goals, life history and preferences", including "customary routine", "psychosocial well-being" and "activity pursuit" [sourced: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/483.20, 42 CFR 483.20(b)(1)]. So life history is named in federal rule. 42 CFR 483.21 itself (care planning) does not contain the words "history" or "life story"; it requires person-centred plans and services that are "culturally-competent and trauma-informed" [sourced: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/483.21]. Activities must be "based on the comprehensive assessment and care plan and the preferences of each resident" and directed by a qualified professional [sourced: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/483.24, 483.24(c)]. F656 is the comprehensive-care-plan tag [sourced-secondary: search extract of CMS/IPRO material]; F679 as the activities tag is from memory and [unknown: not verified this session].
- The requirement is satisfied by the MDS/RAI instrument and an intake interview. No rule requires a narrative, a book, photos or a third-party tool. A regulatory mandate therefore supports a free form or an EHR field, not a paid programme.
- Memory care: no national requirements [sourced-secondary: search extract of Scripps Gerontology Center 2019 report]. Iowa requires "a complete assessment of the resident applicant's physical, mental, social and" behavioural status before admission to a dementia unit [sourced: https://www.legis.iowa.gov/docs/iac/rule/06-11-2014.481.57.7.pdf]. New York and Indiana ask for social or pre-dementia personal history in guidance or forms [sourced-secondary: search extracts]. A 50-state count of such rules: [unknown].
- Hospice: counselling must include an organised bereavement programme, available "up to 1 year following the death of the patient" [sourced: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/42/418.64, 418.64(d)(1)]. The brief says 13 months; the regulation says one year (13 months is a common practice figure, not found in the rule). Medicare does not pay for it: "the statute prohibits Medicare payment for these services", and bereavement and volunteer costs sit in non-reimbursable cost centres; including them would cut the 2022 aggregate margin by at most 1.3 and 0.3 percentage points [sourced: MedPAC March 2025, endnote 24]. Legacy projects are not named in 418.64 and are not separately reimbursable; they are customary, funded from the per diem, volunteers or philanthropy.
- Size of the hospice bereavement cost pool: about $334M per year, or about $257 per hospice death [estimated: 1.3% x $25.7B; divided by 1.3M deaths in hospice from estate.md; mixes a 2022 margin effect with 2023 spend and treats margin points as share of revenue]. Most of it is counsellor salary. A legacy product competes for a slice of this.
- Evidence base, reminiscence therapy for dementia (Cochrane, 22 studies, 1,972 people): no important effect on quality of life overall (SMD 0.11, 95% CI -0.12 to 0.33); probable slight benefit in care homes (SMD 0.46, 0.18 to 0.75; 3 studies, 193 participants); cognition SMD 0.11 "of doubtful clinical importance"; MMSE +1.87 points; communication SMD -0.51 (negative is better) [sourced: Woods et al. 2018, PMID 29493789, via https://eutils.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/entrez/eutils/efetch.fcgi?db=pubmed&id=29493789&rettype=abstract&retmode=text].
- Dignity therapy RCT (441 randomised, 326 analysed): "No significant differences were noted in the distress levels" on primary outcomes; patients reported it more helpful and better for family on secondary self-report (p<0.0001) [sourced: Chochinov et al. 2011, PMID 21741309, same E-utilities endpoint].
- Reading: the evidence supports "residents and families like it" and a small care-home quality-of-life effect. It does not support a clinical or cost-saving claim that would unlock payer reimbursement.

### Q5. Sales reality

- No public sales-cycle or contract-value statement from LifeLoop, August Health or Sage was found: [unknown]. Evidence is indirect.
- Deal shape: enterprise rollouts are announced per operator, e.g. 38 communities (Compass, 2026-10-06, URL above) and 53 communities (Merrill Gardens, May 2026) [sourced-secondary: search extract of https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/merrill-gardens-selects-lifeloop-to-advance-a-hospitality-forward-resident-experience-powered-by-social-wellness-302761809.html].
- Pilot pattern: one operator ran a 90-day pilot across 10 communities with three vendors before choosing [sourced-secondary: search extract of https://seniorhousingnews.com/2025/04/30/cutting-through-the-noise-how-senior-living-operators-find-evaluate-new-tech-partners/]. One vendor-side account: 6-8 months from first contact to a pilot, then a 3-month trial before any rollout [sourced-secondary: search extract of https://www.agetech-ventures.com/p/12-the-hard-truth-about-scaling-agetech, 2026-02-19; body is behind a subscribe wall; single anecdote]. Consultant guides give 60-120 days discovery plus 60-180 days demo and pilot, and 30-90 days rollout per community [sourced-secondary: search extract of https://www.blockconsults.com/guides/senior-living-technology].
- PointClickCare Marketplace: listing requires the Marketplace partner programme, technical validation, production review, per-facility customer approval and an annual minimum integration commitment; fees are not public [sourced-secondary: search extracts of https://marketplace.pointclickcare.com/s/ and an older partner agreement]. Fee schedule: [unknown]. Yardi and MatrixCare marketplaces: not researched, [unknown].
- Staff constraint: assisted-living turnover 71.9% in the 12 months to 30 September, with 39% of new hires leaving within 90 days (Activated Insights) [sourced-secondary: search extract of https://www.mcknightsseniorliving.com/news/assisted-living-staff-turnover-falls-to-lowest-level-since-2024-first-90-days-remain-key-risk, 2026-11-12; headline and date verified by fetch]. A different survey gives 34.5% for 2025 [sourced-secondary: search extract of https://seniorhousingnews.com/2026/01/30/assisted-living-wage-growth-slows-turnover-improving/]. The two disagree by a factor of two; either way any product that needs trained staff to interview residents must be retrained continuously.

### Q6. Family as payer inside the institution

- France (Famileo): where the home is a partner, the establishment pays and families use it free; examples include a group offering it "à tous les résidents et à leurs proches" and a home offering free access from 2025-01-01 [sourced-secondary: search extracts of https://www.groupeafp.com/famileo-pour-tous/, https://www.ehpad-agen.fr/actualites/15240_notre-etablissement-vous-offre-la-gazette-famileo, https://arche-ambroise-pare.fr/famileo-une-nouvelle-facon-de-rester-connecte-avec-vos-proches/]. Families outside partner homes pay about €5.99 per month [sourced-secondary: search extract of https://www.lagazettenews.fr/famileo-tarifs/]. So the care-home channel is institution-paid and acts as distribution; the money is in family subscriptions outside it. Revenue per subscribing family is about €54 per year [estimated: €14M / 260,000; 2024 revenue over a late-2025 family count].
- US: no example found of a community billing families an ancillary fee for a life-story or legacy programme: [unknown, one extended search]. Ancillary charging itself is entrenched (care-level points, e.g. $2,340 on top of $7,895 monthly rent; a $16 million settlement over one operator's point system) and resented: almost half of surveyed families met unexpected add-on fees [sourced: https://kffhealthnews.org/aging/dying-broke-extra-fees-drive-assisted-living-profits/, 2023-11-20]. A new family-billed add-on enters a litigated, reputationally sensitive fee environment.
- Family direct purchase exists at consumer prices: LifeBio $149-$699 (above).

### Q8. Falsification: do these programmes stay tiny?

- Scale after two decades: LifeBio about $2M (2023). Revenue per employee about $42K [estimated: $2M/48], which indicates a labour-heavy service (story teams, phone callers), not software margins.
- Pivots: Storii to EHR; MemoryWell to hospice then apparent inactivity. Both founders were quoted in 2017 selling life stories to facilities; neither business is doing that at scale in 2026.
- Staff cannot sustain it: an integrative review of 13 studies concludes implementation "will require education, time and resources and a commitment from service providers and managers" [sourced: Doran et al. 2019, PMID 30431682, E-utilities]. The NIHR feasibility study in six care homes reports "lack of detected effect for most people with dementia; and questions about implementation" [sourced: Gridley et al. 2016, PMID 27559566, E-utilities]; a companion account says two of six homes withdrew after management turnover and that lack of dedicated time and money impeded delivery [sourced-secondary: search extracts of york.ac.uk and shu.ac.uk papers].
- Free substitutes and bundling: LifeLoop's Life Story feature; StoryCorps Legacy; Famileo's cahier de vie inside a €55 per month subscription; Everplans voice legacy.
- "Free marketing rather than budgeted software": no direct evidence either way: [unknown]. Indirect: no activities-budget survey exists, forum anecdotes put consumables at $9-$23 per resident per month, and LifeBio's $40 per resident per year sits inside that.
- Ceiling arithmetic. Facilities: 56,207 [estimated: 41,465 + 14,742]. Residents: 2,256,424 [estimated: 1,016,424 + 1,240,000]. LifeLoop's 4,700 communities is at most 8.4% of facilities [estimated: 4,700/56,207; overstated because LifeLoop's count includes Canada, independent living and adult day]. At that penetration and observed prices of $40-$150 per resident per year: $7.5M-$28.3M [estimated: 2,256,424 x 8.36% x $40 and x $150]. At 100% penetration: $90M-$338M [estimated]. To reach $100M at 8.4% penetration needs about $530 per resident per year [estimated: $100M / 188,600], three to thirteen times any institutional price found.
- What would change the answer: a payer-reimbursed code (none exists), or a model where the institution is distribution and families pay (the Famileo pattern), which returns the idea to the consumer variants already capped in SCORECARD.md.
