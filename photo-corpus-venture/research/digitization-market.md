### Q9. Falsification — is the market shrinking or saturated?

Evidence **for** saturation / fragility:
1. Photomyne paying subscribers 411k → 408k → 402k over 12 months [sourced: H1 2026 report §1.2]; MAU −19% June-on-June and −22% from Dec 2025 to Jun 2026 [estimated: §1.4 table, Q7]; new-user cash −15% [estimated: 2.8/3.3 − 1, §1.5]; share price at the bottom of its 52-week range with market cap shown as "−44.7%" over an unlabeled period [sourced: stockanalysis.com, 2026-11-02, aggregator]; CFO leaving [sourced: H1 2026 report §2.4]; still loss-making ($1.5M net loss H1 2026) [sourced: H1 2026 report §4.1]. Revenue growth is price and deferred-revenue recognition (Q1).
2. Legacybox shows a persistent complaint pattern (18% 1-star on Trustpilot; 97 BBB complaints in 3 years) concentrated on lost media and involuntary subscription charges, and its newer brands have thin or poor review footprints (Southtree 1 Trustpilot review; Kodak Digitizing 2.6 from 5) [sourced: Q2]. No layoffs found [unknown — absence of evidence]. Customer-milestone velocity is **not** evidence either way: only a floor of ~180k/yr can be derived (Q2).
3. The stock of prints is finite and its holders are old: Photomyne's own deck frames the opportunity as the US 45+ share rising from 34% (2000) to 42% (2024) [sourced: Q3 2025 deck slide 14 citing data.census.gov]. Boomers (80%) value photo keepsakes more than Gen Z (65%) [sourced: Mixbook/YouGov]. The "3.5 trillion" figure Photomyne and ScanMyPhotos cite is a 2011 estimate of *all photos ever taken*, not prints [sourced: Q3 2025 deck footnote citing petapixel 2011]. A print-volume decline figure ("42B prints in 2013, a third below 2008") circulated in round 1 from a search snippet of a Smithsonian Archives blog; the page returned 403 on every attempt and the attribution (IDC vs PMA/InfoTrends) could not be checked, so it is **withdrawn** [unknown]. Photomyne's filing itself says "c.85% of MAU have not yet purchased a subscription" — a large unconverted pool in its own installed base, not a saturated one [sourced: 2025 annual report §7.4.3.1].
4. Price compression: Ancestry now lists $0.49/photo as the standard price with a $20 kit, and the sister brand Southtree lists 25¢ (13¢ on promotion), undercutting Legacybox's ≈$1.28/print kit maths [sourced: Q3; southtree.com] [estimated: customers.md Q1(d)].

Evidence **against** (market still growing):
1. Photomyne cash collections +39% in 2025, +8% H1 2026; US annual subscribers +17%; 83% auto-renew [sourced: Q1].
2. iMemories doubled EBITDA 2022–2025 and "exceeded planned growth objectives" — a strategic sale, not distress [sourced: landoncapital.com, 2025-07-31]. (The "largest acquisition by revenue" quote is unverified and not counted.) Earlier ownership history (Shutterfly → founders) [unknown].
3. Mediafix 273M media converted; Legacybox 700k items/week capacity; Photomyne 599M photos backed up — the category's cumulative numbers keep rising [sourced: Q8, Q2, Q1].

**Verdict:** the US mail-in and app segments look mature and price-competitive. The only disclosed P&L is Photomyne's — $17.0M revenue, $19.6M cash collections, loss-making [sourced: Q1]; Legacybox, Southtree/Kodak Digitizing and iMemories revenue are [unknown], so no per-vendor revenue range is asserted. Growth where it is visible comes from pricing and attach subscriptions, not new households; Ancestry's entry makes the scan itself a loss-leader for tree subscriptions. The wedge is real but it is a **customer-acquisition channel** that three incumbents already own, not an open market.
