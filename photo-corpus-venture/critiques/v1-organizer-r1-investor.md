# Critique — v1-organizer, round 1, investor lens

Written 2026-11-03. Fresh-context critic. Read, in order: SCORECARD.md, variants/v1-organizer.md, every file in research/ and research/assumptions/. Did not read CHANGELOG, other variants, or other critiques.

## Stance

Cold seed investor. Default is no. I care about who pays, how much, why now, the wedge, the distribution, what must be true for a $1B+ outcome, and whether the founder is kidding themselves about platform risk. Allergic to storage economics, to consumer products that need behaviour change, and to small channels dressed up as markets. Ceiling, Distribution and Platform risk are scored hardest.

One-line read: this is the most honest variant I have seen in this project and the least investable. The author has moved the migration to the one party already paid to do it, priced the tool sensibly, kept storage off the balance sheet, and then written down, in their own words, that the ceiling is "$3–12M ARR" and that "this variant is a profitable niche tool or a channel step, not a standalone $100M company." I agree with their arithmetic. A seed cheque needs a story to $100M; the founder has pre-emptively withdrawn it.

## Most damaging objection

How many Mac-based, multi-source digital-consolidation projects does an active Photo Managers member actually run per year?

The entire revenue model hangs on 30 per seat. The association's own survey says members serve 1–3 clients a month across every service they sell — print boxes, scanning, photo books, home-movie conversion, digital organizing — and does not list cross-account consolidation among those services at all (A3.md item 7). Three in four members earn under $50,000 a year and a third are side-gig (pro-channel.md Q1). If the true number is 5–8 consolidation projects per active seat, the seat-year is $500–620 not $1,260, the association-wide project flow is a few thousand not 11,000–17,000, the vault funnel shrinks in proportion, and the "honest" $3–12M ceiling becomes $1–4M. The founder cannot answer this today: the variant marks the share of organizer time that is machine-able as [unknown] and the revenue mix as [unknown]. Until there is a measured count from at least 30 members, nothing downstream of it can be believed, and even the optimistic version is a $10M business.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2).** Self-declared. The seat business tops out under $10M at 100% penetration of every adjacent association summed with unknown overlap; the vault and scanning-licence lines are each sub-$2M on the variant's own assumptions; and the only route past that is a consumer-direct product whose CAC the research puts at $65–90 paid-UA against a $30–60 tolerable ceiling (sizing.md Q4). The organizer channel does not lower consumer CAC; it lends credibility to a few hundred installers.

## Three questions for the founder

1. Have you run one real three-source client project (Takeout + Apple Photos library + a drive) through a prototype end to end, with the organizer's touch-time measured before and after and every input item traced to an output decision? What was the ledger accounting rate, and how many of the "missing" were a wedding?
2. Name five Photo Managers members who bill fixed-fee or per-source, have seen the workbench, and have agreed to pay a deposit for a $390 annual seat. If none, what did the $1,500 focus group say when you asked "what would make you switch", and did anyone say "nothing, I bill by the hour"?
3. You wrote that this is "a profitable niche tool or a channel step, not a standalone $100M company." Which is it, and if it is a channel step, what is the consumer-direct product it steps toward, and why does a trained base of a few hundred organizers lower that product's CAC below the $30–60 the research says is tolerable?
