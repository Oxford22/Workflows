# Track R13 — Paid family-photo artifacts for grandparents: Wi-Fi frames and printed journals

**Bottom line (five lines).**
1. Frames are a proven nine-figure category, paid once by the adult child: Nixplay filed $57.9M revenue for FY2021 [sourced: S13, SEC]; Skylight was reported at $75M for 2021 [sourced-secondary: S8] and now claims "$300M+" [sourced: S9, company job listing, unaudited], most of it from a kitchen calendar, not the grandparent frame.
2. Printed family journals are a proven eight-figure category in exactly one country: Famileo, €15.3M in 2025 after ten years, growing ~9%/yr [sourced-secondary: S25; estimated: 15.3 ÷ 14.0]. No journal operator anywhere has a sourced nine-figure revenue. Neveo is ~€2M [sourced: S26].
3. The US shows no journal traction: Famileo's US iOS app has 110 ratings against 5,322 in France [sourced: S5]; US clones report 520 and 1,000 families [sourced: S48, S49].
4. "Automatic" selection is table stakes (Recently 2015, Google 2020, Chatbooks, FamilyAlbum, Apple Intelligence 2025) and the two journals that scaled are deliberately manual; frames at $59–149 with no subscription already do the "photos to grandma" job.
5. Verdict: the evidence does **not** support a nine-figure outcome for a printed-journal venture. It supports a €2–15M business in a decade, with thin gross margin, unless the US reaches French penetration at 1.7× the French price — for which there is no evidence.

Date of research: 2026-11-24. All URLs accessed 2026-11-24 unless stated. Currency as printed (€, $, £); no FX applied. Estimator sites (Growjo, Latka, Owler, RocketReach, ZoomInfo, Grips, ARR Club, Asinsight) are labelled and never relied on alone.

## 1. Questions asked

(1) Aura, Skylight, Nixplay: revenue, units, funding, prices, subscriptions, storage policy, gift share, ratings, seasonality, 2025–26 products, retail. What do they prove about adult children paying? (2) Printed journals: Famileo, Neveo, Recently, Chatbooks, Lalo, Kindred, Tiny Prints, Timeshel, Groovebook — facts and outcomes, dead and alive. (3) Mechanics and unit economics: price vs print + postage; margin, churn, how photos are chosen. (4) Auto-assembly: who already does it, what reviews say, differentiator or table stakes. (5) US demand evidence: device use, print preference, spend, senior-living programmes, US traction of Famileo-style products. (6) Platform and incumbent risk: Shutterfly/Snapfish/FreePrints, FamilyAlbum prints, Amazon, Apple, Google. (7) Falsification: do recurring-print subscriptions churn or die; have frames commoditised the job.

### Q3. Mechanics and unit economics

- **How photos get in.** Famileo and Neveo are manual: each relative posts a "message" (photo plus caption) in the app; the plan is metered in messages (20/28/36) or photos (30) [sourced: S21, S27]. Layout and print are automatic ("se fabrique presque tout seul") [sourced: S21]. Nothing is pulled from the camera roll.
- **US postage (Notice 123, effective 2026-10-04).** First-Class large envelope (flat), retail: 1 oz $1.69, 2 oz $1.98, 3 oz $2.27, 4 oz $2.56; stamped letter 1 oz $0.82 [sourced: S44]. A 20-page 8.5×11 booklet is five 11×17 sheets ≈ 71–89 g ≈ 2.5–3.2 oz before the envelope [estimated: 5 × 0.1206 m² × 118–148 gsm], so the 3–4 oz flat rate applies: $2.27–2.56. Commercial presort rates for scattered single addresses, and whether a per-family journal may travel as Marketing Mail, [unknown — not resolved].
- **US print cost.** Blurb magazine, 8.5×11: "$7.00 for one copy of a 20-page magazine", $0.20 per extra page, volume discounts from 10 copies [sourced: S43]. Mixam PrintLink saddle-stitch: "$5–$8", formerly "$5 for 32 to 48 pages" [sourced-secondary: S45]. Mixam, Peecho, Gelato, Cloudprinter contract quotes [unknown — JS calculators; Lulu "discontinued its saddle-stitched option for distribution" per S45]. Famileo's and Neveo's per-copy cost [unknown].
- **Price vs cost.** At public one-off prices a 20-page US journal costs $9.27–9.56 to print and post [estimated: $7.00 + $2.27–2.56] against Famileo's $9.99 — no margin. A journal business therefore depends on contract digital print at a fraction of POD list. Scenario, not a forecast: print $2.50 [assumption], postage $2.27, card fees $0.60 [assumption: ~3% + $0.30] → COGS $5.37, gross margin 46% at $9.99 [estimated].
- **Operator statements.** Famileo: thin B2C margin, B2B better because homes print (see Q2) [sourced: S19, S24]. Famileo B2C revenue per family ≈ €53–56/yr ≈ €4.4–4.6/month, vs €4.99 ex-VAT list [estimated: 76–80% of €15.3M ÷ 220,000; 2025 revenue vs mid-2026 family count]. Neveo ≈ €100/yr per subscriber [estimated above]. Groovebook: 70 cents on $2.99 [sourced-secondary: S32].
- **Churn.** Famileo, Neveo: [unknown]. Chatbooks: see Q2 (49,000 cancels in 2024; 150K in 2025; unverified).

### Q7. Falsification

- **"Recurring print dies or churns fast."** Supported. Groovebook dead (2022); Kindred Prints dead; Google's first trial dead in four months; Recently left the monthly magazine for one-off books; Timeshel has 308 ratings in twelve years; Chatbooks cancelled 150K subscriptions in 2025 by its own designer's account [S32, S37, S41, S5, S30]. Counter-evidence: Famileo has grown every year for ten years and Neveo is profitable [S19, S26] — both mail to a **third party** (the grandparent), where cancelling is a visible act of withdrawal. The survivors are gifts, not self-purchases.
- **"Frames have commoditised the job at a one-time price."** Supported. $59.48 Frameo frames lead Amazon unit sales; Aura and Skylight advertise "No Subscription" in the product title [sourced-secondary: S17]; Nixplay's attempt to add a fee produced ~1.2% paid and a lawsuit [S14, S16]. Residual room for paper: 21% of 65+ do not own a smartphone [estimated: 100 − 79, S47] (irrelevant to a frame, which needs only Wi-Fi and a relative to set it up), care-home residents, and homes without Wi-Fi [unknown share].
- **"Adult children pay for grandparent artifacts at scale."** Supported for one-time hardware; supported for a €5.99 journal in France and Belgium; not supported for a $10–17 journal in the US.
- **"A journal can reach nine figures."** Not supported. Scenario only: French-level penetration in the US at US prices ≈ 1.4M families × $120/yr ≈ $168M [estimated: 280,000 × ~5 (US/France population ratio, assumption) × $9.99 × 12]. Against it: ten years and a care-home seed channel to reach 280,000 in France; +9% growth now; 2% US/France rating ratio.
