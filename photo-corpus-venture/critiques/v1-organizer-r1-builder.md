## What breaks at 10k users

- **There are not 10k users.** 7,800 is the sum of every adjacent association (pro-channel.md Q6, overlap [unknown]); 460 active TPM practitioners is the realistic first-year universe. "10k" is past the TAM, which is the Ceiling problem restated.
- **Support**, not compute: every September Apple changes Photos.sqlite (A2.md: four breakages in 2026) and every organizer project in flight that week stalls. The variant budgets "an engineer-month every September" as fixed cost and $50/seat/yr support as "a guess" for a user base the association describes as 90%+ women, 40+, finding Mylio "complicated". Mylio's answer to the same users was to bundle paid human setup (pro-channel.md Q3).
- **Data-loss liability** is the product: the organizer's reputation rides on the ledger, and a 99.5% ledger on a 50k-item project is 250 silently-decided items per project. osxphotos' own clean run reported "missing: 547" of 49,231. One lost wedding album in a 700-person community is a channel-wide event.
- **Legal exposure sits on the customer.** On-device templates give the *vendor* the G.T. v. Samsung defence (face-regulation.md Q1), but the organizer is a business computing face geometry of non-consenting relatives for a commercial purpose on their own Mac, in IL/TX/WA where the incumbents (Google, Amazon, Adobe) switch the feature off. The review-screen consent arrives after collection. The vendor stores names + MWG rectangles + e-signatures server-side — whether rectangles plus a name are "biometric" is [unknown]; "no case found" (face-regulation.md). Selling a BIPA-shaped workflow to a channel of sole proprietors is selling them the lawsuit.
- **Google round-trip at scale**: if the quota is anything like 10k requests/day per project, 10k seats × 30 projects × 50k items is not a backend problem, it is a Google partner-program conversation, which is "[unknown — no Google page found]" (A1.md).

## Most damaging objection

**"Show me one real client whose Google Photos or iCloud account ended the project with fewer items than it started with."** The variant's deliverable is "a clean library back into Apple Photos / Google Photos", but the family's account still holds the 50k messy originals. `appendonly` cannot see, delete or dedupe against them (platform-access.md Q4), the repaired files are byte-different so Google will not collapse them, and Apple Photos has "no completely lossless way to merge" (competitive.md Q1). Unless the organizer deletes the old set by hand in the destination UI, the tool converts 20 hours of dedupe into 20 hours of deletion and a client with twice the photos. The only deliverables that avoid this are the drive + XMP (which the family will not use) and the hosted vault (which v1 makes optional). If the founder cannot answer this with a shipped round-trip on one real account, the "destination-neutral" positioning is the weakness, not the strength.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (2).** The variant does the math and concludes $3–12M. Economics (5) and Buildability (6) are the dimensions a builder would push on next, but they are fixable with a cut spec and a time-and-motion study; the ceiling is structural to the channel.

5. "BIPA professional photo organizer face recognition client photos Illinois liability" / "small business face tagging Illinois lawsuit 2025 2026" — is the organizer, not the vendor, the collecting entity, and has any small studio been sued for face-tagging client photos?

## Three questions for the founder

1. Have you sat with one organizer through one real Takeout + Mac consolidation, stopwatch running? Where do the >20 hours actually go — unzip/dedupe/name (machine-able) or scanning prints and talking to the client (not)?
2. What does the family get on day 30 for a Google-only client: a re-upload into the same account they must then clean by hand, a drive they will not open, or the vault? If the vault is the real deliverable, why is it optional?
3. Who owns the Photos.sqlite reader on 2027-09-15, and what is the plan for the first September Apple ships a change that cannot be followed in a week — or encrypts the database?
