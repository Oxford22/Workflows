Net: ~14 line items; 2 platform-gated (FamilySearch read, write), 2 research-grade unknowns (VLM print dating, handwriting OCR), 1 policy-gated design (BIPA e-sign), 1 fragile reverse-engineered dependency (Photos.sqlite), and the remainder is honest engineering that already exceeds 2 engineers × 4 weeks because the "from current.md" engine does not exist yet.

**Smallest thing that proves the core loop (what I would actually build in 4 weeks):** Mac app; GEDCOM in; one folder of scans in; on-device AdaFace clustering; user names one photo of each ancestor; propagate across the archive; era filter from a cheap VLM decade guess with a visible confidence; XMP out. Measure: (a) what fraction of distinct faces in the box map to any GEDCOM person at all, (b) of those, what fraction are tree-deceased, (c) VLM decade MAE on the ~100 prints the genealogist can date. Those three numbers decide whether the tree is a lever; none of them needs FamilySearch.

**Ugliest technical risk (my pick, sharper than the variant's):** the candidate set is the GEDCOM, and family print boxes are full of people who are not in it — friends, neighbours, in-laws, collateral lines never entered — while the tree is heavy on direct-line ancestors who were photographed rarely. No research file measures GEDCOM coverage of photo subjects. If coverage is low, era-ranking has nothing to rank against and the "≥40% resolved" target is unreachable regardless of face-model quality.

## What breaks at 10k users

- **Compute/storage: nothing.** 10k × 100 MB previews ≈ 1 TB on R2 ≈ $15/mo; captions $2.5k one-time at Haiku 5.5 std; vectors $500/mo (unit-economics.md Q3–Q4). Cousin uploads of originals (un-costed in the variant) are single-digit TB at most.
- **Support.** The variant budgets $5/member/yr and calls it "the real COGS and a guess". The cohort is 55+ (instarestoration survey, low quality; Pew 65+ 79% smartphone), the inputs are GEDCOM + Takeout + Photos.sqlite + scanner folders + a consent flow, and the nearest vendor (Mylio) concluded users "get tripped up sooner or later" and now sells human setup at $240/yr (pro-channel.md Q3). At $25/yr support, contribution falls to ~$32 (53% web, ~42% via App Store) and payback at $35 CAC goes to ~13 months — survivable on annual terms only.
- **Monthly plan.** $6.99/mo exists in the price list. On RevenueCat's 48% first-monthly renewal the expected life is 1.9 months and max CAC $3–9 (unit-economics.md Q7). Ship annual-only or accept that monthly buyers are unacquirable.
- **FamilySearch obligations.** AI Utilization Disclosure Addendum terms unread; Memories API quotas and rate limits [unknown] ("free at quota" is the variant's estimate with no pricing page); 10k users pulling public Memories of their deceased tree persons is exactly the traffic pattern a certification review will ask about. Revocation kills the only free storefront and the only write API at once.
- **Legal exposure.** On-device templates deleted on withdrawal keeps the keeper's device inside G.T. v. Samsung — but (i) Doe v. Apple's faceprint subclass survived on a sync theory, so any template leaving the Mac (Family tier, 6 members; iPhone + Mac) must be shown never to be in the company's control; (ii) the server holds names + MWG rectangles for consented living people and a consent ledger — whether "rectangle + name" is a biometric identifier is [unknown] (current.md Platform risk; no case found); (iii) the "reference-face corpus for deceased persons" (Compounding asset #2) is a server-side face database unless it is shipped to every device — see Objection below.
- **Data-loss liability.** Low: originals stay in the family's own storage. The one exception is cousins' uploads of prints the keeper does not hold, stored single-copy on R2; small but should be in the retention policy.

## The single most damaging objection

**The value in session 1 — and therefore the kill criterion — depends on reference faces the product cannot obtain within its own demo.** The variant's resolve step names a cluster only when a reference face exists: either public FamilySearch Memories (access gated behind Solutions Program acceptance with unknown lead time and an AI-disclosure addendum whose terms have not been read) or the user hand-naming portraits first. The 4-week acceptance test ("≥40% of clusters resolved to a tree person in session 1") therefore either runs on the gated integration (not available on day 1 by the variant's own plan) or measures user labour. Without reference faces the product is, in the variant's words, "Photomyne with a tree", and Photomyne's measured tagging rate is 0.10 tags per scan (digitization-market.md Q6). Compounding this: the only "tree-agnostic" way to pre-seed reference faces — the proposed corpus built from users' confirmations and public FamilySearch Memories — is a facial-recognition database assembled from internet-sourced images, which face-regulation.md Q4 says fails the "targeted" test under EU AI Act Art. 5(1)(e) and which sits server-side in contradiction with "templates never leave the machine". So the one asset that would make day-1 resolution work is the one the design says it will not hold.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (3)**, tied with Platform risk (3). The variant concedes it ("$100M does not pencil from this pool alone"), and research/genealogy-market.md §3 reached the same number independently (3% attach ≈ $4.5–7.2M; 10% ≈ $15–24M). Platform risk is the structural tie: Ancestry already ships deceased face match and AI dating over the same corpus and can lift its deceased-only rule by decision.

## Unknowns to research before the next round (searchable)

1. **FamilySearch Solutions Program lead time and AI addendum text.** "FamilySearch Solutions Provider application approval time" / "FamilySearch AI Utilization Disclosure Addendum" (developers.familysearch.org; partner blog posts by Related Faces, MemoryWeb, Storied on how long certification took). Does the addendum permit on-device face embeddings computed from Memories images?
2. **GEDCOM coverage of photo subjects.** Any study of who appears in family photo collections vs. who is in the owner's tree (search: "family photo collection identification rate relatives named" / "who is in family photo albums study"); Photomyne's 3.3M taggings — tags per distinct person; Ancestry Face Match hit rates from user blogs ("Face Match found" posts).
3. **VLM zero-shot decade estimation on scanned prints and handwriting OCR on photo backs.** "VLM date estimation DEW benchmark" / "Gemini Flash photo year estimation accuracy" / "handwritten caption OCR scanned photo back accuracy TrOCR vs GPT"; or a 100-print hands-on test with known dates, which the research says is the only way.
4. **Mac vs Windows share of paying genealogists.** RootsMagic / Family Tree Maker Mac-vs-Windows sales split; Legacy Family Tree (Windows-only) user base; TPM/NGS member device surveys. v1 is Mac-first for a cohort whose platform is [unknown].
5. **Attach rate of paid photo add-ons among genealogy subscribers.** MyHeritage Photo plan subscriber count or Complete-subscriber attach; why AI Time Machine was sunset (take-up vs. cost); Related Faces customer count. These bound the 1–10% attach band that drives every ceiling number.

## Three questions for the founder

1. On one real corpus you own, what fraction of distinct faces in the box map to any person in your GEDCOM, and what fraction of those are recorded as deceased? If you have not run this, why is "≥40% resolved in session 1" the acceptance number?
2. Two engineers get one input path in 4 weeks. Which ships: the Mac app on the unbuilt current.md engine (Photos.sqlite + Takeout repair), or a scans-folder-only build with no Apple/Google ingest? The spec claims both plus an iPhone path it never schedules.
3. Where does the "reference-face corpus for deceased persons" live? If server-side, how is it not a biometric database (mis-marked living persons under BIPA; EU users under GDPR Art. 9; AI Act 5(1)(e) if seeded from public FamilySearch Memories at scale)? If on-device only, how is it delivered to a new user's Mac, and what size is it?
