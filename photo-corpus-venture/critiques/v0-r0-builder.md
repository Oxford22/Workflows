## The single most damaging objection

**Show me one iPhone — not a Mac — with a ~100 GB iCloud-optimized library that completed an unattended full export to your server through `PHBackgroundResourceUploadJobExtension`, with the measured wall-clock, the `jobLimit` you hit, and the share of iCloud-only originals that failed to download.**

This is the first thing every user must do before any value exists, and on the platform where 79% of iPhones already run iOS 26 the evidence says nobody can do it today: twelve months after Apple shipped the extension, Immich closed the request as "not something we're likely to add anytime soon", Ente says it "requires us to adopt a very different architecture", Google Photos and Dropbox help pages (Dropbox updated 2026-09-17) still say keep the app open and plugged in overnight, and the only adopter is a 31-star app marked "experimental" that needs an extra proxy server and works "in the background after the initial sync" — i.e. not for the initial sync (research/assumptions/A2.md §1–2; research/platform-access.md Q2). Apple publishes neither the inflight job limit nor the per-invocation runtime, so the docs cannot answer this; only a run can. If the founder cannot produce that run, the migration premise does not exist on iOS and the venture is a Mac-and-Takeout tool for hobbyists.

## Weakest dimension

**Buildability (3)** — tied numerically with Wedge, Distribution and Ceiling, but it is the gating one: nothing else can be tested until a real family library crosses from an iPhone and a Google account into the product without babysitting. The next round should either (a) produce the measured run above, or (b) re-scope the variant so migration is not the first step (index-only over PhotoKit via Apple's own iOS 27 `LanguageModel` plumbing, or a Mac-first/Takeout-first organizer tool), which also changes the Economics row.

## Builder notes

### Smallest thing that proves the core loop (4 weeks, 2 engineers)
- Input: one real Mac Photos library (osxphotos, `--download-missing` off, originals local) + one relative's Google Takeout zip set.
- Pipeline: immich-go-style sidecar matching → perceptual dedupe → SigLIP 2 B/16 embeddings (~172 img/s on a desktop CPU per research/ai-why-now.md Q1) → AdaFace (MIT) faces + HDBSCAN → pgvector on one $48 DigitalOcean box (~1,000 users disk-resident per research/unit-economics.md Q4).
- Output: a remote MCP server behind OAuth, added to Claude Free via the prefilled install link (research/assumptions/A6.md Evidence For #1); query "photos of Grandma in the 1970s".
- Cost to index the 22,000-image reference user: ~$0.85 mid tier (research/unit-economics.md Q3).
- What this proves: the query loop. What it does not prove: iPhone migration, Google-side migration without a technical user, cross-account face identity, or that a non-technical family member will do any of it.

### Ugliest technical risks, ranked
1. iOS unattended export (above). Secondary: iCloud-original fetch throttling is outside the app's control and undocumented (research/platform-access.md Q1).
2. Google ingest. Takeout is the only bulk path: 50 GB archives, 7-day expiry, 5 downloads, inconsistent sidecar names, shared-album contributors' photos absent, one user "retried a Google Takeout export over about two weeks" (research/assumptions/A1.md; research/customers.md Q1(g); research/assumptions/A3.md Evidence For #2). Expect a support ticket per family.
3. Face identity across relatives and ages. Google: "Face labels are private to each account and aren't shared across accounts." Apple: no People API on iOS at all. So every identity must be re-derived, and the multi-decade child→adult case is unsolved (research/ai-why-now.md Q3). The honest product claim is "assisted, user-confirmed merging", which means a confirmation UI and a human doing the work.
4. macOS fallback fragility: osxphotos is one maintainer's reverse-engineered reader; four breakages on macOS 26/27, one AMFI issue open since 2026-07-18 (research/assumptions/A2.md §6). Shipping this to consumers means owning that reader and re-engineering it every September.
5. Face-data regulation: Everalbum's default-on face recognition drew an FTC order to delete "any models or algorithms developed" with users' biometric data (research/competitive.md Q5). Consent has to be explicit from day one.

### What breaks at 10k users
- Storage. 10k users × 100 GB = 1 PB. Single-copy B2: ~$6,950/mo. Ente-pattern 3-copy replication (the architecture "ownership that outlives any account" actually implies): ~$17,700/mo (research/assumptions/A5.md Evidence Against #2). If 10k is signups rather than payers, RevenueCat's 22.2% Photo & Video trial→paid gives ~2,200 payers ≈ $11k/mo gross at $4.99 — single-copy storage for everyone's library eats ~63% of that before fees, support, or compute. Free-tier policy is therefore a survival decision, not a growth lever (Everpix: 7,000 payers of 50,000 users, "covered variable costs but not fixed costs", dead).
- Support. Every item in risk list 1–4 is a ticket, and the failure modes are silent (osxphotos "missing: 547"; FamilySearch batch ops "you dont get an error - it simply doesnt work"; Immich stalls). With 2 engineers, migration support is the headcount plan.
- Data-loss liability. The pitch is permanence for irreplaceable photos; single-copy object storage on one provider does not deliver it, replication breaks the margin (research/assumptions/A5.md), and the one incumbent whose business is consumer backup needed ten years to cross 60% GAAP gross margin.
- Compute does not break: indexing 10k reference users is ~$8.5k one-time at the mid tier and <$0.01/user/mo incremental (research/unit-economics.md Q3–Q4). Storage is the only cost that matters, which is exactly the founder's own "storage company" fear, and the research confirms it holds.

## Three questions for the founder

1. Have you run a real ~100 GB, iCloud-optimized iPhone library through the iOS 26.1/27 Background Resource Upload extension end to end? What `jobLimit` did you hit, what was the per-invocation runtime limit, what was the wall-clock, and what fraction of iCloud-only originals failed to download? If not: what is your migration path for the Mac-less iPhone user, and how many nights plugged in is "acceptable"?
2. Take ten real families' Google Takeout exports (multi-zip, shared albums, partner-shared photos). What is your measured ingest success rate, how many photos lose metadata or go missing, and who answers the ticket when a parent's 547 photos are "missing"?
3. "Cluster faces across generations": when the corpus contains Grandma at 8 in a 1962 print and Grandma at 70 in a 2024 iPhone photo, what does the demo do? If the answer is human-confirmed merging, how many confirmations does a 20,000-photo library require, and is that labor the user's, yours, or a photo organizer's?
