## The single most damaging objection

**Nobody has been found paying for the thing v0 sells, and the vendor closest to the customer who tried to charge for it shows no buyers.**

Concretely: research/assumptions/A3.md sub-claim (iii) is "broken as stated". The only merge-specific products on the market are PowerPhotos ($39.95 one-time), KinPhotos ($49/yr, no customer count, no founding date) and Mylio Family ($480–$732/yr, bundled with 5 hours of human help, no subscriber count, vendor blog silent since 2023-07-04). The adjacent money (scanning $0.49–$1.28/print, organizers $50–$150/hr, Ancestry $20–40/mo, Tinybeans $75 ARPU) is all for a different job: digitization, labor, records, forward capture. And on 2026-10-07 Immich v3.3.0 shipped cross-user shared people for free to the one population that demonstrably spends hours on this (A3.md Against #3).

If the founder cannot name a household that paid more than $100 in the last twelve months specifically to consolidate two relatives' libraries into one, with cross-account faces, and say what they paid and to whom, then the "customer: unspecified, business model: unspecified" lines in v0 are not placeholders, they are the answer. Everything downstream (storage GM, CAC, ceiling) is arithmetic on a zero.

## Weakest dimension

**Wedge (2)**, tied with Ceiling (2). I name Wedge because it is the one the founder can change and because the other low scores are consequences of it: both v0 wedges require the user to migrate a multi-GB library off Apple/Google *before* any value, which is the behavior change I am allergic to, which the founder's own "known killers" list leads with, and which A2 shows no shipping app has made unattended on iOS a year after Apple provided the API. Ceiling cannot be raised until a wedge that money already flows through (scanning order, organizer engagement, estate event) replaces "migrate everything first".

## Three questions I would ask the founder

1. **Who paid, how much, for what?** Name one household, in the last twelve months, that paid more than $100 to merge two relatives' photo libraries with cross-account face identity. If the answer is "an organizer's client", why does an organizer billing $50–$150/hr for 20+ hours hand those hours to your software instead of keeping them? (A3.md #7; customers.md Q1(e))

2. **Which company are you?** If you store the originals with replication (the only way "ownership that outlives any account" is credible, and what Ente actually does with 3 copies on 3 providers), your gross margin at the $4.99/200 GB market price is 53%, 47% via the App Store, and negative at full fill (A5.md Against #3). If you go index-only to get 86%, the originals stay in iCloud/Google, and Apple's iOS 27 Foundation Models framework now lets any app run Claude or Gemini over that library without you (A4.md Against #5). Pick one; tell me why the other half of the pitch survives.

3. **What is your measured iOS onboarding completion rate?** For a 40,000-item library with iCloud-optimized originals on an iPhone running iOS 26.1+, using Apple's Background Resource Upload extension: how many nights plugged in, what `jobLimit` did you hit, what fraction of users finished? Ente, Immich, Google Photos and Dropbox all still document "keep the app open" as of Sept–Oct 2026 (A2.md Against #1). If you have not run this, the family-archive wedge has not been tested at all.
