## Failed or overstated claims (variant number vs research)

1. **"~15 min for 30k photos on-device"** (variant Wedge, citing ios-feasibility Q3 Scenario A). ios-feasibility.md Q3 states the 15-minute figure is "a floor built from batch-1 model benchmarks, not a central estimate"; central is ~38 min, ~57 min derated, and iOS 27 serializes ANE calls so parallelism cannot recover it. The variant quotes the floor as the number. Overstated.
2. **"Funeral Video Maker $39/$99/$249; pro services $299–$850 (secondary)"** (variant "What they pay today", and the $149 kit anchor "between DIY tribute video $39–$249"). estate.md Audit log item 3: these figures were "never fetched; removed from Q5, Q8, funnel and numbers tables" and replaced with talkpix's $75–$300 funeral-home add-on (often included), $150–$600 pro, $800–$2,000 complex. The variant cites withdrawn numbers to anchor its core price. Failed claim.
3. **"Ingest compute: embeddings + faces on the heir's Mac or a RunPod 4090"** (variant Economics) vs **"templates for living people are computed on-device and synced only as ciphertext"** (variant Platform risk) and **"server-side face matching for living people in IL/TX/WA/EU" explicitly NOT in v1**. face-regulation.md Q1/Q7: the Samsung defence holds only if the company never possesses the template; the Apple court rejected encryption as a shield at summary judgment. A RunPod face pass over the decedent's 22,000-photo library computes templates of every living relative in it. The two sections contradict each other; the economics row assumes the architecture the legal row forbids. Internal contradiction, not a research mismatch, but it means one of the two numbers (COGS or legal exposure) is wrong.
4. **"Tukios Aftercare-By-Text $99/mo ... 500 families/yr; AI monitors replies"** — the variant's own fetch, not in any research file. research/estate.md Q6 carries only Aftercare.com ($25/family, $150/month) and Tukios' tribute-video upload link. Unverifiable from the corpus; treated as plausible but untested.
5. **"Aftercare touchpoints ... two years ago the evidence for it was a mailed card"** — estate.md Q6 gives no launch dates for either vendor ("Launch dates [unknown]" per the variant itself). The "why now" framing rests on an unknown.
6. **"the deceased's phone can be erased"** as a Day-7 output — estate.md Q2: self-export via the passcode must happen *before* a Legacy Contact request is filed, because the original account "no longer works" once approved; and the one documented court-order case recovered nothing from reset devices ("Apple will not (apparently cannot) give you access to anything on any of their devices"). The product must gate "erase" on a 100% ledger match and sequence passcode-export before filing; the spec does not say so. Omission with data-loss consequences.
7. **"carries Apple's existing People names across where the Mac path is used"** — true only for the Mac path (platform-access.md Q3), and ios-feasibility.md Q5 puts Mac ownership at 36–40% of recent Apple buyers (lower among 85+ decedents, [unknown]). The headline kit promise ("with the deceased and the family named") is, for most estates, re-derived by our face pipeline, not carried over.

## What breaks at 10k estates/yr (builder's view)

- **Support.** Variant budgets $15/estate ($150k, ~2 FTE). Every ingest path has a silent failure mode (osxphotos "missing: 547"; Takeout split-zip sidecars; iCloud.com 1,000-item gaps; Apple export contents undocumented) and the customer is bereaved and non-technical. The one sourced comparable for consumer data recovery, DriveSavers, sits at 2.8/5 on Trustpilot (estate.md Q5). Expect multiples of $15.
- **Data loss.** 90-day single-copy B2 transit for photos the heir may not hold anywhere else once Apple's legacy account is deleted or the phone is erased. A5.md: single-copy storage "does not credibly deliver" permanence. One lost estate is a lawsuit and a headline.
- **Legal.** Server-side AdaFace on a decedent's library = templates of living relatives on our servers. face-regulation.md: BIPA $1,000–$5,000 per person (post-2024 per-person), CUBI AG action, WA MHMDA private right; IL+TX = 12.7% of the US population; Apple is at trial on exactly the sync theory. The on-device constraint must hold for every ingest path including web upload, or the Illinois estate is off-limits.
- **Cost.** Compute does not break ($8.5k/yr). Egress is free on B2 up to 3× storage; a 450 GB estate exported to four heirs (1.8 TB) exceeds that and bills $0.01/GB — minor.
- **Sequencing error.** Heir with passcode files a Legacy Contact request before self-exporting → original account "no longer works" (estate.md Q2) → permanent loss attributable to our checklist.

## Most damaging objection

**Have you requested your own Apple Data & Privacy photo export and opened it?** What was inside — albums, People names, edited/original pairs, Live Photo pairs — how many gigabytes in how many files, and how many days did Apple take to prepare it? The entire $149 Recovery Kit sells the ingestion of that bundle; Apple's page says only "original formats" and "14 days to download", and its file guides explicitly exclude photos (estate.md Q2, §4 item 2). This is a $0, one-week experiment available to any Apple account holder, and the variant schedules it for Week 4 of a build. If the bundle is a flat dump of originals in 5 GB chunks that a 70-year-old cannot pull down in 14 days, the kit is a checklist — the variant's own kill criterion — and the conversation is over.

## Weakest dimension

**Ceiling (3).** The variant's own arithmetic tops out at $16–55M US and concedes it is "the natural front door for the federation plan". Compounding asset (3) is tied, and for the same structural reason: the product is designed to hand everything back to the heirs' Apple/Google libraries, so neither the archive nor the revenue compounds.
