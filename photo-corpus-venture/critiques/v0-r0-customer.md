# v0 critique — round 0 — stance: TARGET CUSTOMER

Scored 2026-11-03 by a fresh-context critic who read SCORECARD.md, variants/v0.md and every file in research/ (nothing else). I have not seen the author's reasoning.

## Stance

I am the person v0 says it serves: a non-technical adult with a large multi-year photo library, already paying Apple $2.99/mo for 200 GB of iCloud (or Google $1.99–4.99 for Google One). My parents are on iPhone, my brother is on Android, there are boxes of prints at my mother's house, and I have thought once or twice — after a funeral, during a phone upgrade — that "someone should put all of this in one place". I am not going to run a command line. I will not cancel iCloud, because it backs up my phone. What I care about: would I actually do the first step, what do I give up, who do I trust with my family's faces, what happens if the startup dies, and would I tell my sibling about it. Pain, Wedge and Distribution are scored hardest.

## Most damaging objection

**The first step is a multi-night babysitting job that no one with a product team has made unattended, and I have to get my relatives to do it too.** Research/assumptions/A2.md shows that twelve months after Apple shipped the iOS 26.1 Background Resource Upload extension, Ente ("Do not swipe Ente away from the app switcher"; issue #7127 still open 2026-08-24), Immich (discussion #23245 closed as "not something we're likely to add anytime soon"; a user stalled at 1,286 of 220,791 files), Google Photos ("Keep the Photos app open and your phone plugged in") and Dropbox (help page dated 2026-09-17: "keeping the Dropbox mobile app open") all still tell users to keep the app open for a large initial backup. The alternatives are Google Takeout (one user "retried a Google Takeout export over about two weeks", A3.md) or a Mac running a reverse-engineered Python CLI that broke three times on macOS 27 (A2.md item 6). v0 lists "migration friction" as a known killer and offers no answer. If the founder cannot show one non-technical relative who completed step one on a phone without supervision, there is no wedge, no referral loop and no family archive — and I keep paying iCloud regardless, so this is a second bill for a job I have not yet seen done.

## Weakest dimension

**Wedge (2).** Both candidate wedges require behaviour change before any value is delivered, and the research found no segment paying for the merge itself (customers.md §3; A3.md sub-claim iii).

## Three questions I would ask the founder

1. Walk me through, tap by tap, what my 72-year-old mother does on her iPhone to get her 40,000 iCloud photos into your product — and how many nights it takes with the phone plugged in. Which of the apps you studied has done this unattended, and if none, why will you be first?
2. Name one household (or one segment with a count) that has paid money — not for scanning, not for storage, not for a baby-photo app — to merge two relatives' libraries into one. Mylio Family charges $480–732/yr and bundles five hours of human help; KinPhotos charges $49/yr; neither publishes a customer. Why will I be different?
3. If you shut down in eighteen months, what do I hold? A ZIP with the faces named and the voice notes attached, in a form that Apple Photos or Google Photos will read back — given that neither platform imports People/face labels today (platform-access.md Q5)? And in the meantime, why would I pay you when Siri AI and Ask Photos already search my photos for free?
